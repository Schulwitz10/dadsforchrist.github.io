Love Her as Christ

A man who loves his wife as Christ loved the church spends himself for her good, and that love steadies the marriage, teaches the children, and gives the home the shape of Jesus.

Men, a house can be provided for and still be unloved. A wife can tell when love never takes the shape of a cross. You can share a roof and spend yourself on your own comfort. This word is for husbands, for fathers, and for the man who will be one. The love you practice now is the love a marriage will receive, and the love children will learn to call Christlike.

Paul will not let a man lower the standard.

“Husbands, love your wives, even as Christ also loved the church, and gave himself for it; That he might sanctify and cleanse it with the washing of water by the word, That he might present it to himself a glorious church, not having spot, or wrinkle, or any such thing; but that it should be holy and without blemish. So ought men to love their wives as their own bodies. He that loveth his wife loveth himself.”

Ephesians 5:25–28

Christ loved the church by giving himself for her. He did not wait until she was easy, and he did not offer less when the cost rose. He gave his life to wash her by the word and present her holy. Paul puts that love on a husband. It is not a feeling you wait for. It is a giving of yourself, on ordinary days, for her good. You are not called to remake her into a copy of your preferences. You are called to spend yourself so she can flourish under Scripture, and to stand under that word first.

“For no man ever yet hated his own flesh; but nourisheth and cherisheth it, even as the Lord the church:”

Ephesians 5:29

You notice when your own body is weary, and you tend it. Paul says the Lord treats the church that way, and a husband is to treat his wife that way. Nourish her. Cherish her. The proof is quiet: your tone, a task taken from her hands, and drawing near when she is worn thin.

Paul says it once more.

“Nevertheless let every one of you in particular so love his wife even as himself; and the wife see that she reverence her husband.”

Ephesians 5:33

Love her as yourself. That charge is for every husband, and it trains the man who is not married yet. Selfish habits do not leave on a wedding day. How you speak when you are tired, how you serve when no one thanks you, and how you repent when you are wrong are the love you would bring home. If God gives you a wife, let her meet a man who has already been learning Christ.

A love she can point to

Give her the first of your attention when you come through the door. If you were sharp, apologize without pinning her faults to the end of the sentence. Ask what would serve her today, and do it without keeping a receipt.

Carry a real weight with her. Ask what is heavy before you say what you need, and take one task she has held alone. If she is discouraged, stay near instead of rushing a fix that mainly helps you feel finished.

Let your strength shelter her. Speak with the honor you would want on your own name. When a hard truth must be said, say it as a man who is for her, and keep a gentle voice. A child who hears you learns that love can be honest and safe.

Open the Bible and stand under it first. Read a short passage with her when she is willing, and pray it in plain words. Let the text correct you before you offer it to anyone else. Scripture is water for you both, not a tool for winning.

Then put the ledger down. Christ did not love the church because she had paid him back. Love her when she thanks you and when she does not. A marriage shaped by the gospel keeps faith instead of keeping score.

What the house receives

A wife loved this way can tell the truth. Hard evenings will still come. She should not have to wonder whether she is cherished only when she is easy. Peace grows where her good matters more than your comfort.

Children learn this before they can explain the chapter. A son watches how you treat the woman you vowed to love, and he stores it as manhood. A daughter watches whether strength is safe. They see a preference laid down for their mother, and a man who can say he was wrong. In that home Jesus is not only named. He is resembled.

If you are not a husband or a father yet, begin while the cost is your habits. Serve when no one notices. Tell the truth without crushing someone. Let the word wash you. The man you become now is the man a family would have to live with.

The Savior who gave himself for the church still goes after one soul.

Who is my one? Begin with your wife, if the Lord has given you one, and love her as Christ loved the church. Then ask the Lord for one soul outside your door. Read the mission, wear the Reach One bracelet as a reminder to reach one soul, or give to the work.

A prayer

Father, thank You that Christ loved the church and gave Himself for her. Forgive me for loving my comfort first. Teach me to nourish and to cherish, to put her good ahead of my ease, and to let Your word wash this life. If You have given me a wife, help me love her as my own body. If You have given me children, let them see Jesus in the way I love. Make me a man who gives himself, at home and for the one You send me to reach. In Jesus’ name, amen.
