Returned to Bless His House

First Chronicles 16:43 says that all the people departed, every man to his house, and David returned to bless his house. On Thanksgiving Day, after the worship and the crowds, the ministry that matters most is going home: being present, speaking blessing over a wife and children, leading thanksgiving at his own table, staying soft when he is met with conflict, and remembering the one man who has no house to go home to.

Men, today is Thanksgiving. The service, the crowd, and the long table somewhere else will pass, and every man will go to his own house. What you do at that door is the ministry that remains. This word is for husbands, for fathers, and for the man who will be one. Scripture in this post is quoted from the King James Version.

“And all the people departed every man to his house: and David returned to bless his house.”

1 Chronicles 16:43

The chapter is a day of public thanks. David blesses the people in the name of the LORD, and a psalm of thanksgiving is given. Then the people depart, and David returns to bless his house.

After the celebration, the worship, and the big public moments, the ministry that matters most is going home. Be present. Speak blessing over your wife and your children. Lead thanksgiving at your own table.

“Enter into his gates with thanksgiving, and into his courts with praise: be thankful unto him, and bless his name.”

Psalm 100:4

Carry that thanks through your own door. Be thankful unto Him, and bless His name where your household can hear it.

What she receives

A wife can carry a long holiday and still wonder whether her husband has a blessing for her. If God has given you a wife, thank Him for her by name, out loud. Tell her she is loved, and take one weight so the day does not rest on her alone. A man who is present gives her what a public speech cannot. If you are not married yet, practice that voice with the people beside you.

What the children learn

Children learn what a man believes when the guests are gone. Sit with them. Bless each child by name, and thank God out loud. One verse and one prayer at your own table teach them that faith lives in the house. If no child is listening yet, bless the people in the room.

When you are met with contempt

David returned to bless his house, and he was not met with peace. Second Samuel 6 tells the same homecoming.

“Then David returned to bless his household. And Michal the daughter of Saul came out to meet David, and said, How glorious was the king of Israel to day, who uncovered himself to day in the eyes of the handmaids of his servants, as one of the vain fellows shamelessly uncovereth himself!”

2 Samuel 6:20

He came home to bless his household. Michal met him with contempt. You may finish the worship and walk into a sharp word, a cold room, or a quarrel that was already waiting. Do not shut down. Go home. Stay soft. Keep blessing.

“Husbands, love your wives, even as Christ also loved the church, and gave himself for it;”

Ephesians 5:25

Christ loved the church and gave Himself for her. First Peter 3:7 calls a husband to dwell with his wife according to knowledge, giving honour unto her, so that his prayers are not hindered. Stay in the room. Answer gently. Give her honour when the hour is tense. The call to bless your house does not wait for a warm welcome.

What the home becomes

When a man is present and blessing, his wife is not left to carry the holiday alone, and the children hear the name of the Lord in the voice they know best. You cannot control every answer at the door. You can return, stay, and let your mouth give life.

Who is my one?

“What man of you, having an hundred sheep, if he lose one of them, doth not leave the ninety and nine in the wilderness, and go after that which is lost, until he find it? And when he hath found it, he layeth it on his shoulders, rejoicing. And when he cometh home, he calleth together his friends and neighbours, saying unto them, Rejoice with me; for I have found my sheep which was lost. I say unto you, that likewise joy shall be in heaven over one sinner that repenteth, more than over ninety and nine just persons, which need no repentance.”

Luke 15:4–7

Some men have no house to return to today. The Shepherd still goes after the one until he finds it.

Who is my one? Name him before the night is over. If you have a house, open it, and let him hear a blessing under your roof. If you are that man, you are not forgotten. Be present where you are, then reach one man who has no house to bless. Read the mission, wear the Reach One bracelet as a reminder to reach one soul, or give to the work.

Before this night ends

  1. When the public part of the day is over, go home on purpose to bless your house.
  2. Lead a short thanksgiving at your own table. Name the Lord, and name one mercy out loud.
  3. If God has given you a wife, thank Him for her where she can hear it. If the hour is tense, stay soft and keep giving her honour.
  4. If you have children, bless each one by name. If you do not yet, bless the people beside you.
  5. Name one man who has no house to go home to. Call him, or open your door, before you sleep.

A prayer

Father, the people have departed, every man to his house. Teach me to return and bless mine. If You have given me a wife, help me love her as Christ loved the church, and help me stay soft when I am met with conflict. If You have given me children, let them hear a blessing from my mouth tonight. Show me one man who has no house to go home to, and give me courage to bring him in. In Jesus’ name, amen.
