Before the Sun Goes Down

Husbands and wives face anger together. A man who will not let the sun go down on his wrath keeps bitterness from settling on his wife, his children, and his home.

Men, some of the hardest battles a husband and wife face begin when the day should already be done. A sharp word lands. Anger stays in the room. Silence gets called peace. This word is for husbands, for fathers, and for the man who is neither yet. If you have a wife, she is not the enemy. If children share the house, they are learning what a man does with heat. Scripture in this post is quoted from the King James Version.

Paul tells you what to do with anger before night comes.

“Be ye angry, and sin not: let not the sun go down upon your wrath: Neither give place to the devil.”

Ephesians 4:26–27

Be angry, and sin not. The feeling is not itself the sin. What you do with it can be. To let the sun go down upon your wrath is to take the heat to bed and give the night time to harden it. Paul says that gives place to the devil. A quarrel can be honest. Wrath kept overnight becomes a foothold. The question is not who wins the point. It is whether the anger will be named and laid down before you sleep.

You can tell yourself you will deal with it when you are less tired. Morning comes, and the word is still between you. She has carried it through the night. In this battle, a present man goes back before the house is dark. Say what you did. Ask what she heard.

“Let all bitterness, and wrath, and anger, and clamour, and evil speaking, be put away from you, with all malice: And be ye kind one to another, tenderhearted, forgiving one another, even as God for Christ’s sake hath forgiven you.”

Ephesians 4:31–32

Bitterness is anger that has been saved. Clamour is the voice raised until it fills the kitchen. Evil speaking is the cutting line, including the one said about her when she is not there to answer. Malice wants her to hurt because you do. Put them away. Be kind, tenderhearted, and forgiving, because God, for Christ’s sake, has forgiven you.

That choice lands on a wife, on children, and on the whole house. When kindness returns to the room where you were sharp, she can stand with you against real trouble instead of bracing against you. A son learns that strength can turn around. A daughter learns that truth can stay tender. A home that will not keep wrath overnight stops making everyone live around the last argument. If you are not a husband or a father yet, put bitterness away with the people who already know your temper.

“A soft answer turneth away wrath: but grievous words stir up anger.”

Proverbs 15:1

A soft answer does not call a real problem small. It tells the truth without adding a wound. Grievous words stir anger up again. You can be right about the fact and wrong in the sentence. Hear her through before you explain yourself.

“Wherefore, my beloved brethren, let every man be swift to hear, slow to speak, slow to wrath: For the wrath of man worketh not the righteousness of God.”

James 1:19–20

The wrath of man does not produce the righteousness of God. Heat will not grow holiness in a wife or a child. Be swift to hear, slow to speak, and slow to wrath. Jesus will not separate that peace from worship.

“Therefore if thou bring thy gift to the altar, and there rememberest that thy brother hath ought against thee; Leave there thy gift before the altar, and go thy way; first be reconciled to thy brother, and then come and offer thy gift.”

Matthew 5:23–24

First be reconciled, then bring your gift. If she has something against you, go to her. Say it plainly: “I was wrong in the way I spoke. Please forgive me.” If she needs time, do not punish the wait. If you are not married yet, go to the person you wounded before you pray.

A way to begin this week

  1. Before you sleep, ask whether the sun is going down on your wrath. If it is, go and name it.
  2. Lay down one weapon: the loud voice, the cutting remark, or the cold silence.
  3. Offer one soft answer where you want to win, and hear the whole concern first.
  4. Ask forgiveness, or give it, in a single sentence.
  5. If a child saw the anger, let that child hear the apology.
  6. If you are not yet a husband or a father, keep the same rule with family and friends this week.

A prayer

Father, thank You for forgiving me for Christ’s sake. Forgive the wrath I have kept, the grievous words, and the silence I have used to punish. Make me swift to hear, slow to speak, and slow to wrath. Help me to be kind and tenderhearted, to forgive as Christ forgave me, and to make peace before the sun goes down. Guard the wife and children You have entrusted to me, and the home You may yet give. In Jesus’ name, amen.

Who is my one? Begin with the person your anger reached. Then ask the Lord for one soul outside your door.

“What man of you, having an hundred sheep, if he lose one of them, doth not leave the ninety and nine in the wilderness, and go after that which is lost, until he find it?”

Luke 15:4
