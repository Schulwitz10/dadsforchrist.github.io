Provoke Not Your Children

Ephesians 6:4 and Colossians 3:21 call a man not to provoke his children to anger, but to bring them up in the nurture and admonition of the Lord. When he is present and patient, the children are not discouraged, his wife is not left alone in the home, and the house learns gentleness instead of wrath.

Men, a child can be fed and clothed and still grow up under a hard voice. A man can provide for a house and still be missing from the room that forms it. This word is for husbands, for fathers, and for the man who will be one. Scripture in this post is quoted from the King James Version.

“And, ye fathers, provoke not your children to wrath: but bring them up in the nurture and admonition of the Lord.”

Ephesians 6:4

“Fathers, provoke not your children to anger, lest they be discouraged.”

Colossians 3:21

Do not stir them to wrath. Do not push them into anger. Bring them up. A provoked child can be discouraged, and a discouraged heart expects little good from the man who should have been safe. The verses name fathers, and the work belongs to every man who will stand in a home. If children are in your care, this is your charge today. If they are still ahead, the spirit you are forming now is the spirit they will meet.

Anger, harshness, and absence

Provocation is a voice that stays sharp after the point is made, a joke that cuts, and a silence longer than the offense. It is correction with no nurture beside it. It is living under the same roof without being with them. Anger teaches by heat. Harshness tells the truth in a way that crushes. Absence does its own harm, even when a man calls the empty chair providing. You cannot be sharp all week and repair the house with one soft hour.

“Wherefore, my beloved brethren, let every man be swift to hear, slow to speak, slow to wrath: For the wrath of man worketh not the righteousness of God.”

James 1:19–20

The wrath of man does not work the righteousness of God. Swift to hear means you let them finish. Slow to speak means the first words are not spent on your pride. Slow to wrath means heat does not do the teaching. If no child is in the house yet, practice that pace with the people already near you.

Nurture and admonition, in one man

To bring them up is a long staying. Nurture feeds, guards, and comes close. Admonition warns and points a child toward the Lord. Warning without nurture grows cold. Softness without admonition leaves no path. God asked for both in the same man.

“Like as a father pitieth his children, so the LORD pitieth them that fear him.”

Psalm 103:13

A man who learns that pity does not sneer at weakness. He tells the truth about what is wrong, and he stays near while he tells it. Gentleness is not a small view of sin. It is strength that will not crush the one it is called to raise.

What gentleness does in the house

A son who is not provoked to wrath can bring a failure without bracing for a blast. A daughter can ask what she does not know. Discouragement loosens when a man is steady, because love and correction share one voice.

A wife feels that spirit. When her husband is patient with the children, she is not the only gentle one, and she is not left to repair them after he leaves the room. The marriage is helped by that same gentleness. A man who will not rule his anger with them will have a hard time keeping it from the way he speaks to her.

The house changes tone. People talk, and they stay. Admonition can be heard, because it does not land on a heart waiting for the next blow. Come home in more than your body. Hear one thing they want you to know, and let them see a face that is not already angry.

The one

“What man of you, having an hundred sheep, if he lose one of them, doth not leave the ninety and nine in the wilderness, and go after that which is lost, until he find it?” (Luke 15:4). “And when he hath found it, he layeth it on his shoulders, rejoicing” (Luke 15:5). And “joy shall be in heaven over one sinner that repenteth” (Luke 15:7). Who is my one? It may be the child you have been short with, or one man whose anger is costing him his house. You cannot save that one. Christ does. Be present, be patient, and go.

Three things to do this week

  1. Today, before you correct anyone in your care, hear the whole story. If no child is in the house, give that same slow answer to one person near you.
  2. This week, name one way you have been harsh or absent, say so in plain words, and stay long enough to listen.
  3. Ask the Lord who your one is, and take one gentle step toward that person before the week ends.

Who is my one? Begin with the person under your roof who has felt your anger more than your patience. Then ask the Lord for one soul beyond your door. Read the mission, wear the Reach One bracelet as a reminder to reach one soul, or give to the work.

A prayer

Father, You pity Your children, and I have been quicker to wrath than to compassion. Forgive the words that provoked anger, and the hours I was gone while I was still in the house. Make me swift to hear, slow to speak, and slow to wrath. Teach me to bring them up in the nurture and admonition of the Lord, and keep a gentle spirit ready if that gift is still ahead. Do not let them be discouraged. Send me after the one. In Jesus’ name, amen.
