Carry It Together

Galatians 6:2 calls a man to bear one another’s burdens and so fulfil the law of Christ. When a husband asks what his wife is carrying and takes a real weight beside her, she has a partner in the battle, the children who watch learn that love stays and lifts, and the home shares a load that was meant for two.

Men, a husband and a wife go through battles that belong to both of them. Money gets thin. A child is struggling. Sickness stays. Grief sits down at supper, and the work of a home starts again every morning. You can share a roof and still leave her holding the weight. This word is for husbands, for fathers, and for the man who will one day be a husband. Scripture in this post is quoted from the King James Version.

“Bear ye one another’s burdens, and so fulfil the law of Christ.”

Galatians 6:2

Bear it. Put your shoulder under what another person is carrying until the load is lighter. In a marriage, the nearest burden is your wife’s. She is the first person this word asks you to help.

Some loads are loud: a hospital stay, a layoff, a fight still in the room. Others stay quiet. She may be carrying the calendar, the worry over a child, a bill she has stopped mentioning, or a sadness she manages so the house can keep its shape. Ask her before you decide she is fine.

“And the LORD God said, It is not good that the man should be alone; I will make him an help meet for him.”

Genesis 2:18

An help meet is a companion fitted to him. She was given so that he would not be alone, and she was not meant to be alone either. When a husband steps back from the hard parts and calls them her work, he leaves her in the aloneness God named as not good.

Love that takes the weight

“Husbands, love your wives, even as Christ also loved the church, and gave himself for it;”

Ephesians 5:25

Christ gave himself. He moved toward the need. A husband’s love takes that shape on ordinary days: the dishes after a long shift, the appointment you drive her to, the night you rise with the child so she can sleep, the hard talk you stay inside. Carry that piece until it is done.

Cast the care, and stay

Some weights are heavier than a husband can lift. A diagnosis. A loss. A fear that will not move on command. You are her husband. Christ is the Savior. Stay beside her, and lead both of you to Him.

“Casting all your care upon him; for he careth for you.”

1 Peter 5:7

Name the care out loud with her, and put it in His hands. He cares for you both. Praying with her is one way you bear the burden. If the week has made you harsh, say so first. A few plain sentences are enough, and the morning after should agree with them.

“Come unto me, all ye that labour and are heavy laden, and I will give you rest. Take my yoke upon you, and learn of me; for I am meek and lowly in heart: and ye shall find rest unto your souls. For my yoke is easy, and my burden is light.”

Matthew 11:28–30

Jesus invites the heavy laden and offers a yoke shared with Him. A husband who has learned of Christ comes near when his wife is weary and takes a side of the work. His meekness makes room for her to tell the truth.

What the house receives

When you ask what she is carrying, and you stay until she is finished, the battle becomes shared.

Children watch before they have words for it. A son learns whether a man steps toward a hard thing or leaves it on his mother. A daughter learns whether a husband’s strength is a shelter. Where a man bears the burden with her, the children learn that love stays and lifts.

If you are not married yet, begin now. Notice what the people near you are carrying. Practice asking, helping, and praying. The husband you will be is being formed by the man you are today.

Carry it with her this week

  1. Ask her what she is carrying. Ask it plainly, then be quiet long enough for a true answer. Let her finish before you offer a plan. If you are not married, ask someone God has set near you, and listen all the way through.
  2. Take something off her plate. Choose one real task she has been holding, a meal, a bedtime, a bill, a drive, or a mess, and carry it this week without being asked twice. Do the whole thing.
  3. Pray with her. Name the battle out loud and cast that care on the Lord together. If she is not ready to pray beside you, pray for her, and let the way you treat her agree with the prayer.
  4. Notice the children watching. Let them see you move toward her load, speak of her with honor, and stay when the evening is heavy. If no child is in the house yet, practice that nearness now, so it is already your way when they arrive.

Who is my one? Begin with your wife, if the Lord has given you one, and bear the burden with her. Then ask Him for one soul beyond your door. Read the mission, wear the Reach One bracelet as a reminder to reach one soul, or give to the work.

A prayer

Father, You told us to bear one another’s burdens and so fulfil the law of Christ. Forgive me for the loads I have seen and left on her. Teach me to ask, to listen, and to take a real weight without keeping score. Where the burden is too heavy for us, teach us to cast our care on You, for You care for us. Give her rest, and make me a man who walks beside her. If children are watching, let them see love that stays and lifts. If a wife is still ahead, make me fit to carry life with her. In Jesus’ name, amen.
