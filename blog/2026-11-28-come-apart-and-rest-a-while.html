Come Apart and Rest a While

In Mark 6:31 Jesus told His tired disciples, “Come ye yourselves apart into a desert place, and rest a while.” On the quiet weekend after Thanksgiving, a man can stop running long enough to actually be with his family: resting without disappearing, slowing down beside his wife, giving his children unhurried time, letting God lead his rest, and making room for the one man who is running on empty.

The holiday rush has a way of leaving a man running on fumes. The travel, the cooking, the cleanup, the shopping, the work that is still waiting on Monday. By the Saturday after Thanksgiving, a lot of us are home in body but somewhere else in mind.

Jesus knew what that kind of tired looks like. After His disciples came back from being sent out, Scripture says:

“And he said unto them, Come ye yourselves apart into a desert place, and rest a while: for there were many coming and going, and they had no leisure so much as to eat.”

Mark 6:31, KJV

Notice what He did not say. He did not tell them to push through. He did not tell them to scatter and recover on their own. He said, “Come ye yourselves apart” — come away together, with Me, and rest.

Men, that is a word for us this weekend.

Rest Is Not the Same as Disappearing

Many men think of rest as escape. We retreat into a screen, a game, the garage, or the couch with headphones on. We call it recharging, but often it is just a quieter way of being gone.

There is nothing wrong with a ball game or a nap. But a man can sit in the same room as his family all weekend and still not be present. Your wife can feel the difference between a husband who is resting beside her and a husband who has checked out. Your children can feel it too.

God built rest into the rhythm of creation, and He did it for the whole household:

“Six days shalt thou labour, and do all thy work: But the seventh day is the sabbath of the LORD thy God: in it thou shalt not do any work, thou, nor thy son, nor thy daughter, thy manservant, nor thy maidservant, nor thy cattle, nor thy stranger that is within thy gates.”

Exodus 20:9–10, KJV

The rest God commanded was not one man hiding from everyone. It was a whole house stopping together before the Lord.

Slow Down Beside Your Wife

The busy seasons are hard on a marriage. Husbands and wives can spend weeks working side by side like coworkers, passing each other with lists and logistics, and never really talking. The battle is not always a fight. Sometimes it is just drifting.

A slow weekend is a chance to come back to each other. Sit with her. Ask how she is really doing after all the hosting and the running. Listen without trying to fix it. If she has carried more than her share of the holiday load, tell her you saw it and thank her for it.

“Two are better than one; because they have a good reward for their labour.”

Ecclesiastes 4:9, KJV

Rest together is part of that reward.

Give Your Children Unhurried Time

Children do not measure love by how busy their father is. They measure it, in large part, by whether he has time for them. Not squeezed-in time. Unhurried time.

This weekend, let them have you without a clock running. Take a walk. Build something. Play the game they keep asking you to play. Let a conversation go wherever it goes. The point is not the activity. The point is that they get their dad, fully there, with nowhere else he would rather be.

“To every thing there is a season, and a time to every purpose under the heaven.”

Ecclesiastes 3:1, KJV

There is a season for working hard. There is also a season for sitting on the floor with your kids. Do not let the first one swallow the second.

Let God Lead Your Rest

True rest is not only a break from work. It is coming back to the One who carries what we cannot.

“Come unto me, all ye that labour and are heavy laden, and I will give you rest.”

Matthew 11:28, KJV

“Be still, and know that I am God.”

Psalm 46:10, KJV

A man who never gets still before God will try to carry his whole house on his own strength, and he will wear out. A man who rests in the Lord can lead his family from a full heart instead of an empty one. Take a few quiet minutes this weekend with your Bible open. Then invite your family into it: read a short passage at the table, or pray together before bed.

Remember the One Running on Empty

Not every man has a quiet house to rest in this weekend. Some are alone. Some are carrying grief, a broken marriage, or a weight nobody knows about.

Jesus told of a shepherd who left the ninety and nine to go after the one that was lost (Luke 15:4–7). Who is your one? Maybe it is a friend who went quiet after the holidays, or a man at church who seems worn out. A text, a call, or an invitation to coffee could be the rest his soul needs.

Practical Takeaways

  • Put the phone in another room for one meal and one evening this weekend.
  • Ask your wife one real question — “How are you really doing?” — and just listen.
  • Give each child unhurried time, doing something they choose.
  • Read one Scripture together as a family, at the table or before bed.
  • Reach out to your one — a man who may be running on empty.

A Prayer

Lord Jesus, You see how tired we get and how easily we drift. Teach us to come apart with You and rest. Help us to be fully present with our wives and our children, not just in the room, but in heart. Give us stillness before You, so we can lead our homes from a full heart. And show each of us the one man who needs us to reach out. In Jesus’ name, Amen.
