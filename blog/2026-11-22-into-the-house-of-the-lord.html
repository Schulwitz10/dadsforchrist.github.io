Into the House of the Lord

Psalm 122:1 calls a man to be glad when he is invited into the house of the Lord, and Hebrews 10:24–25 calls him not to forsake the assembling of God’s people. When he leads his household in and sits under the Word with them, his wife is not left to carry the family’s faith alone, the children learn that worship matters by watching where he goes, and Sunday sets the tone of the week.

Men, it is easy on the Lord’s Day to stay home, or to leave your people at the door and keep the hour. The Lord’s Day is for worship in the house of the Lord, and a man leads by going in with his household and staying there. This word is for husbands, for fathers, and for the man who will be one. Scripture in this post is quoted from the King James Version.

“I was glad when they said unto me, Let us go into the house of the LORD.”

Psalm 122:1

Glad. Let us go is plural. He was not sending the others while he stayed behind. He was going into the house of the Lord with them.

That is the lead to give on Sunday. Get up, go in, and sit under the Word until it is finished. Presence is a seat in the assembly and a Bible open in your hands. It is not a car at the curb. If you have a wife, she should not walk that aisle alone. If you have children, they should see you beside them. If a wife and children are still ahead of you, be in that house now, so the habit is already yours.

“And let us consider one another to provoke unto love and to good works: Not forsaking the assembling of ourselves together, as the manner of some is; but exhorting one another: and so much the more, as ye see the day approaching.”

Hebrews 10:24–25

Some had made a manner of staying away. A man cannot stir others toward love and good works, or exhort them, from the couch. He cannot do it by handing his household off at the door. The assembling is where those words become a life.

Joshua bound his own house to the choice in one line: “as for me and my house, we will serve the LORD” (Joshua 24:15). Say it the night before. Keep it when the alarm sounds.

What she receives

A wife can sit under the Word while the faith of the family rests on her. When you rise and go in beside her, she is not carrying it alone. You are not assigning her the soul of the home and keeping the morning for yourself. She needs you awake through the service, and willing, once you are home, to speak of what you heard. If the morning is hard, say so without making her the obstacle. Then go. Gladness often begins as obedience. If you are not married yet, do not practice absence and hope it becomes presence later.

What the children learn

Children learn that worship matters by watching where a man goes and how he sits under the Word. An open Bible teaches them. A man who only waits for the end teaches them something else. They notice whether Sunday aims the house at the house of the Lord.

You cannot believe for them. You can show them the gathering is worth getting ready for. God commanded that His words be in the heart, and then taught diligently to the children, in the house, on the way, at bedtime, and at rising (Deuteronomy 6:6–7). On the way home, ask what they heard, and answer it yourself. If no child is watching yet, sit under the Word as a man who will be watched.

What the home receives

Sunday sets the tone for the week. “Except the LORD build the house, they labour in vain that build it” (Psalm 127:1). The work of a paycheck, a meal, and a correction is vain if the Lord is not the builder. Pray before you leave. Put the phone away while the Word is preached. Come home and let one line you heard shape one ordinary thing before Monday: grace at the table, a sharp word held back, or time kept to open the Bible again. A house that has been in the Lord’s house does not enter the week unchanged. If you live alone, that same morning still sets your days.

Who is my one?

Jesus told of a man who leaves the ninety and nine and goes after one lost sheep “until he find it.” There is joy in heaven “over one sinner that repenteth” (Luke 15:4–7). Think of one man who has stopped coming, or who has never come. Invite him to sit with you in the house of the Lord. Offer the ride. Save the seat. Stay beside him, and let the morning be worship.

Who is my one? Lead whoever shares your roof. Then invite one man to come to church with you. Read the mission, wear the Reach One bracelet as a reminder to reach one soul, or give to the work.

Go in with them

  1. Settle it the night before. Tell the Lord you will be in His house. If others share your roof, tell them, and lay out what the morning needs.
  2. Go in and stay. Sit with your household if you have one, open the Bible, and remain to the end. If you live alone, take a seat where you can hear, and greet someone before you leave.
  3. Bring one line home. Name what you heard, and let it shape one choice this week.
  4. Invite one man. Pray for him by name, then ask him to come with you and sit at your side.

A prayer

Father, make me glad when they say, Let us go into Your house. Forgive the mornings I stayed home or left my people at the door. Help me assemble with Your people and sit under Your Word. If You have given me a wife, let her know she is not carrying this faith alone. If You have given me children, let them learn worship by watching where I go. Set the tone of our week by this day. Give me one man to invite. In Jesus’ name, amen.
