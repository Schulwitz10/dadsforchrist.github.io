More Than a Paycheck

A paycheck can keep a household, and God calls a father to provide. Children still need his time, his attention, and a man who will lead them to Jesus.

Brother, a paycheck can keep the lights on and the refrigerator full. That work is good. God calls a man to provide, and a father who neglects his household has missed a serious part of his calling. Your children were not given a wage in place of a dad. They were given you. They need your time, your attention, and a man who will lead them toward Jesus. Scripture in this post is quoted from the King James Version.

“But if any provide not for his own, and specially for those of his own house, he hath denied the faith, and is worse than an infidel.”

1 Timothy 5:8

Refusing to care for your own is a denial of the faith. The early alarm and the bill paid on time can be a father’s love. Give thanks if you have been faithful there. A house can still be paid for and missing its father.

“Except the LORD build the house, they labour in vain that build it: except the LORD keep the city, the watchman waketh but in vain. It is vain for you to rise up early, to sit up late, to eat the bread of sorrows: for so he giveth his beloved sleep. Lo, children are an heritage of the LORD: and the fruit of the womb is his reward.”

Psalm 127:1–3

If the Lord is not building the house, early mornings and late nights are in vain. Children are a heritage from the Lord, meant to be held, not only funded from a distance.

Presence is what that looks like on an ordinary Tuesday. At the table, you sit down, the phone stays in another room, and you wait for the real answer about their day. You thank God for the food where they can hear you.

At bedtime, you take a few unhurried minutes on the edge of the bed. You listen for the fear they saved until the house was quiet, pray a short prayer, and bless them before the light goes out. They will remember that you came back.

In prayer, spiritual leadership is a father who opens the Bible and lets his family see that he needs God. Moses told Israel to keep God’s words on the heart, then to talk about them in the ordinary hours.

“And these words, which I command thee this day, shall be in thine heart: And thou shalt teach them diligently unto thy children, and shalt talk of them when thou sittest in thine house, and when thou walkest by the way, and when thou liest down, and when thou risest up.”

Deuteronomy 6:6–7

You cannot teach what you are never home to say. Paul assumes a father is close enough to shape a child’s heart.

“And, ye fathers, provoke not your children to wrath: but bring them up in the nurture and admonition of the Lord.”

Ephesians 6:4

Discipline without a relationship grows harsh. Bring them up by staying for the work, including the hard conversations. A child has failed, or a teenager is angry. You lower your voice, tell the truth without mocking, and stay near enough that correction does not feel like being sent away. Malachi spoke of a mercy our homes still need.

“And he shall turn the heart of the fathers to the children, and the heart of the children to their fathers, lest I come and smite the earth with a curse.”

Malachi 4:6

A turned heart wants to know the children under the roof, not only to pay the bills. Then they have a place to turn back.

Jesus told of a father who was watching the road. The son had wasted everything. He was still a long way off when his father saw him.

“And he arose, and came to his father. But when he was yet a great way off, his father saw him, and had compassion, and ran, and fell on his neck, and kissed him.”

Luke 15:20

The father ran. Compassion moved his feet. That is a present dad: eyes up, ready to meet his child. Our heavenly Father came near in Christ. You are not the savior of your home. Jesus is. Your nearness is one way your family learns what His nearness is like. Children learn that a man’s attention can be steady, and they learn to pray because they have heard their father pray. A wife gains a partner at the table and a voice beside hers at bedtime. The home is warmer because someone is actually there.

If you have been faithful with a paycheck and thin on presence, you do not need a new personality. You need a turned heart and a few ordinary returns this week.

A way to begin this week

  1. One evening, put the phone in another room and sit through the meal. Ask each child one question, and let them finish.
  2. Keep one bedtime. Read a short passage, pray one honest prayer, and bless your children by name.
  3. Ask your wife where she has felt alone, and hear her before you explain your hours.
  4. Have the conversation you have been postponing. Stay kind, and stay until it is finished.
  5. Before work, pray out loud for your household. Three sentences in the kitchen are enough.

A prayer

Father, thank You for work to do and for the people under my roof. Forgive me where I have treated a paycheck as the whole of a father’s calling. Turn my heart toward my children and toward their mother. Teach me to sit at the table, to show up at bedtime, to pray where they can hear me, and to stay for the hard word. Build this house, or my labor is in vain. In Jesus’ name, amen.
