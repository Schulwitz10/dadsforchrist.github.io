Go After the One

Jesus leaves the ninety and nine to go after the one lost sheep. The first mission field for a man is his own home, then the one man who has wandered from God, and being present is how he goes after them.

Men, the one God is sending you after may already share your roof. This word is for husbands, for fathers, and for the man who will be one. Jesus does not wait for the lost to wander home. Scripture in this post is quoted from the King James Version.

“What man of you, having an hundred sheep, if he lose one of them, doth not leave the ninety and nine in the wilderness, and go after that which is lost, until he find it? And when he hath found it, he layeth it on his shoulders, rejoicing. And when he cometh home, he calleth together his friends and neighbours, saying unto them, Rejoice with me; for I have found my sheep which was lost. I say unto you, that likewise joy shall be in heaven over one sinner that repenteth, more than over ninety and nine just persons, which need no repentance.”

Luke 15:4–7

He leaves the ninety and nine and goes after the one until he finds it.

Jesus gives that shepherd His own name.

“I am the good shepherd: the good shepherd giveth his life for the sheep.”

John 10:11

You cannot save a soul. Christ does. You can still refuse to stay comfortable while someone you love is wandering.

Home comes first

A man’s first mission field is his own home. Your wife, if God has given you one, is not a project. She is the nearest heart there. She may have stopped naming what is heavy, and she may wonder whether you will come after her or only keep the house running.

The one may be a child who is drifting. A quiet son can be lost in plain sight. A quiet daughter can learn that you track the schedule and miss her soul. Do not wait for them to come back on their own. The shepherd goes until he finds the sheep.

If that family is still ahead of you, practice this with the person nearest you now.

One man outside the door

Then ask the Lord for the one man who has wandered. It may be a friend who no longer speaks of Jesus, a coworker carrying more than he admits, or a neighbor whose life has come apart. You are sent after one, not after a city. Paul shows how to go.

“Brethren, if a man be overtaken in a fault, ye which are spiritual, restore such an one in the spirit of meekness; considering thyself, lest thou also be tempted. Bear ye one another’s burdens, and so fulfil the law of Christ.”

Galatians 6:1–2

Restore him in meekness, bear his burden beside him, and remember that you can fall the same way.

“Brethren, if any of you do err from the truth, and one convert him; Let him know, that he which converteth the sinner from the error of his way shall save a soul from death, and shall hide a multitude of sins.”

James 5:19–20

If he errs from the truth, go after him. Pursue, pray, and stay. If you have wronged him, say so first.

Present is how you go

Being present is how a man goes after the one. Notice what you have walked past. Put the phone down and listen. Then pursue with a meal, a walk, or a seat on the edge of a child’s bed. Pray before you speak. Pray with your wife when she is willing, and say a child’s name aloud. Pray for that man. Prayer does not replace going, and love does not wait for the lost to come back on their own.

What pursuit does in a house

A wife who is pursued can breathe. She is not left to chase you for prayer or for a conversation that reaches her. A drifting son needs a knock and a man who stays. A quiet daughter needs to be found. They learn that love searches and that failure does not end belonging. You cannot repent for them. You can refuse to let distance set the climate of the house, so the home takes the shape of the Shepherd.

A band on the wrist

The Reach One bracelet is a daily reminder to pray for and pursue one person. When you see it, name that person again. Then notice, go, and pray. Do not wait.

Three things to do this week

  1. Name your one in writing. Start with your wife, or with a child who is distant or quiet, if God has given you either. Then name one man outside your door. If you live alone, name the person nearest your life.
  2. Pray that name each morning before you reach for a message. Ask the Lord to draw that person and to make you present. If you are married, invite your wife into the prayer when she is willing.
  3. Take one step before the week ends. Sit with your wife without a screen, knock and stay with your child, or call that man and ask how he really is.

Who is my one? Begin under your own roof, then go after the one man who has wandered. Read the mission, wear the Reach One bracelet as a reminder to pray for and pursue one soul, or give to the work.

A prayer

Father, You go after the one. Forgive me for staying where it is easy. Make me present: noticing, pursuing, and praying. Help me go after my wife’s heart if You have given me one, and after the child who is drifting or quiet. Give me one man to restore in meekness. Let this band send me again tomorrow. In Jesus’ name, amen.
