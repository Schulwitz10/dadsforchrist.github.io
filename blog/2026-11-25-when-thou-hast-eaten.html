When Thou Hast Eaten

Deuteronomy 8:10–11 calls a man who has eaten and is full to bless the LORD his God for the good He has given, and to beware that he forget Him. The day before Thanksgiving, he leads his household in that remembering: blessing the Lord after the meal, telling his children what God has done, standing beside his wife in thankfulness, and remembering the one man who will sit at an empty table.

Men, tomorrow is Thanksgiving. The table will be full. That is a mercy, and it is the hour Moses warned about. The danger of a full table is not hunger. The danger is forgetting. This word is for husbands, for fathers, and for the man who will be one. Scripture in this post is quoted from the King James Version.

“When thou hast eaten and art full, then thou shalt bless the LORD thy God for the good land which he hath given thee. Beware that thou forget not the LORD thy God, in not keeping his commandments, and his judgments, and his statutes, which I command thee this day:”

Deuteronomy 8:10–11

The warning was not for the hungry days. It was for the day a man has eaten and is full. Bless the LORD your God for the good He has given. Then beware. Forgetting starts when a pleasant meal feels as if it arrived on its own, and no one at the table names the Giver.

“Bless the LORD, O my soul, and forget not all his benefits:”

Psalm 103:2

David speaks to his own soul first. If your heart stays silent, the house will learn silence. Tell your soul the truth, then lead the remembering out loud.

What she receives

A Thanksgiving table is heavy work. A wife can carry the food, the guests, and the dishes while the men around her eat and never bless the Lord where she can hear it. Stand beside her in thankfulness. Do not leave the holiday on her shoulders.

Before the meal, ask what she needs you to hold. Thank God for her by name, out loud. After the plates are cleared, stay and take the work that remains. Thankfulness next to her says the Lord has been good, and it refuses to make her the only one who remembers. If you are not married yet, thank God out loud now.

What the children learn

Children do not learn the works of God from a full plate. They learn them from a man who will not hide them.

“We will not hide them from their children, shewing to the generation to come the praises of the LORD, and his strength, and his wonderful works that he hath done. For he established a testimony in Jacob, and appointed a law in Israel, which he commanded our fathers, that they should make them known to their children: That the generation to come might know them, even the children which should be born; who should arise and declare them to their children: That they might set their hope in God, and not forget the works of God, but keep his commandments:”

Psalm 78:4–7

Show the next generation the praises of the LORD, His strength, and His wonderful works, so they might set their hope in God and not forget. Tell them one true thing He has done: daily bread, mercy when you failed, strength in a hard week. If no child is listening yet, say it anyway. You are learning the voice they will one day hear.

After you are full

Deuteronomy puts the blessing after the meal. “When thou hast eaten and art full, then thou shalt bless.” Grace before the first bite is good. The harder obedience is to bless the Lord when you are no longer hungry. When people start to rise, pause them. Name one benefit. Enjoy the gift and keep His commandments in the same hour.

Who is my one?

“What man of you, having an hundred sheep, if he lose one of them, doth not leave the ninety and nine in the wilderness, and go after that which is lost, until he find it? And when he hath found it, he layeth it on his shoulders, rejoicing. And when he cometh home, he calleth together his friends and neighbours, saying unto them, Rejoice with me; for I have found my sheep which was lost. I say unto you, that likewise joy shall be in heaven over one sinner that repenteth, more than over ninety and nine just persons, which need no repentance.”

Luke 15:4–7

A full table can make a man forget an empty one. Somewhere this week a man will eat alone. The Shepherd goes after the one until he finds it.

Who is my one? Name him before you sit down to plenty. Call him. Invite him to the table. If he cannot come, carry a plate to his door and bless the Lord with him. If you are the man at an empty table, you are not forgotten. Be present under your roof, then reach one man who may have nowhere to sit. At tomorrow’s table

  1. Lead a short grace before anyone eats, and ask the Lord to keep the house from forgetting Him.
  2. If God has given you a wife, ask what she needs you to carry, thank Him for her where she can hear it, and stay so the holiday does not rest on her alone.
  3. When you have eaten and are full, pause and bless the LORD out loud. Name one benefit before anyone rises.
  4. If you have children, tell them one work of the Lord so they might set their hope in God. If you do not yet, tell the people beside you.
  5. Name one man who may sit alone. Call him or invite him before the day ends.

A prayer

Father, when we have eaten and are full, teach us to bless You and not forget. If You have given me a wife, help me stand beside her in thankfulness. Help me show the next generation Your wonderful works, that they might set their hope in You. Bring to mind the one man at an empty table, and give me courage to go after him. In Jesus’ name, amen.
