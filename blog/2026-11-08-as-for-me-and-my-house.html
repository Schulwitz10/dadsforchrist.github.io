As for Me and My House

Joshua 24:15 calls a man to decide, and to lead, that his household will serve the Lord. When he is present in worship, his wife, his children, and his home receive a clear direction in a busy age.

Men, a household does not drift toward the Lord. Work fills the week, and a screen fills what is left. Husbands and wives feel that pull together until Sunday slides and the Bible stays shut. This word is for husbands, for fathers, and for the man who will be one. Someone has to decide whom the house will serve.

Joshua drew the line.

“And if it seem evil unto you to serve the LORD, choose you this day whom ye will serve; whether the gods which your fathers served that were on the other side of the flood, or the gods of the Amorites, in whose land ye dwell: but as for me and my house, we will serve the LORD.”

Joshua 24:15

A crowded week, a glowing screen, or the habit of making faith wait can take a statue’s place. Joshua said, choose you this day. Then he joined his household to the choice: as for me and my house, we will serve the Lord.

The line begins with me. Read before you tell anyone else to read. Pray before you ask your wife to pray. Your decision comes first, and then it sets the direction of the rooms you share.

Serve Him “in sincerity and in truth” and “put away the gods” that compete with Him (Joshua 24:14). A motto on the wall is not that service. Your wife is your partner under the same pull. Lead, so she can see that Jesus is the center of the home, and live the choice beside her.

Worship she can see

A present man is the one who can lead the worship of a house. Be in the room. Put the phone down at the table. Come back to your people before the evening is used up. A wife walks with a husband who is really there. Children learn a faith that shares their rooms.

Lead them to church. “Not forsaking the assembling of ourselves together” (Hebrews 10:25). The Lord’s day is the family saying, in public, whom it serves. Get up, bring them, and sit under the word. If you have no wife and no child yet, be in that assembly now.

Lead prayer and Scripture at home. “And these words, which I command thee this day, shall be in thine heart” (Deuteronomy 6:6). Teach them to your children and talk of them “when thou sittest in thine house, and when thou walkest by the way, and when thou liest down, and when thou risest up” (Deuteronomy 6:7). Read a few verses at the table or at bedtime. Thank the Lord, confess where you failed, and ask Him for the people under your roof. Pray with your wife when she is willing, and let a child hear the name of Jesus.

“Be ye followers of me, even as I also am of Christ” (1 Corinthians 11:1). Repent where they can hear you. Keep a small promise. Choose the Lord when comfort argues.

What that decision does

A wife can breathe when she is no longer alone in tending the soul of the home. Your lead stands with her. Church, an open Bible, and prayer put you beside her when a distracted culture tugs.

Children learn the Lord of the house from what you repeat. A son who sees the Bible open learns that a man lives under God’s word. A daughter who hears you pray learns that strength can kneel. They notice whether you cancel the Lord’s day or guard it. You cannot believe for them. You can make Scripture and prayer the sound of their house.

The home gets a center. Thanks is said at the meal, and the week bends around worship. After a hard word, you have a Lord to return to. Hurry loses the last word, because you kept deciding in front of them.

If a wife and children are still ahead of you, practice now. As for me, I will serve the Lord. The church, the prayers, and the Scriptures of these hidden years teach a future household who its Lord is.

Three things to do this week

  1. Say it on purpose. Tell the Lord you will serve Him. If you are married, tell your wife without blame: as for me and this house, we will serve the Lord. If you live alone, say it over your life and keep it.
  2. Put the Lord’s day on the calendar first. Assemble with God’s people, and lead whoever shares your roof. On Saturday, clear one thing that usually ruins the morning.
  3. Read and pray once where your household can hear you. If you live alone, keep a time you will not skip. Do it again before the week ends.

A house that serves the Lord looks outward. Jesus told of a man who leaves the ninety and nine and goes after one lost sheep “until he find it”, then calls his friends to rejoice. There is joy in heaven “over one sinner that repenteth” (Luke 15:4–7).

Who is my one? Begin under your own roof. Then ask the Lord for one soul outside the door. Pray for that person, encourage that person, and point that person to Jesus. Read the mission, wear the Reach One bracelet as a reminder to reach one soul, or give to the work.

A prayer

Father, You are worthy of this house. Forgive me for serving my hurry. I choose today to serve You. Make me present, faithful in the assembly, and ready to pray and open Your word at home. If You have given me a wife, let us face this age together. If You have given me children, let them see whom we serve. If a family is still ahead, keep me faithful now. Send me to the one who is lost. In Jesus’ name, amen.
