A Place of Refuge

A present father gives his children a refuge, stands beside his wife, and teaches the home whom it serves.

Brother, the people under a roof can tell when a man is truly with them. Children find a refuge in a father who is there. A wife finds a partner in the work of the house. The home takes a direction toward the Lord. This is for husbands and fathers, and for the man who will be one. Scripture in this post is quoted from the King James Version.

Solomon names the gift that the fear of the Lord gives a family.

“In the fear of the LORD is strong confidence: and his children shall have a place of refuge.”

Proverbs 14:26

A refuge is a father whose voice is still in the house on a hard day, whose place at the table is filled, and whose face stays kind after correction. A son learns that strength and safety can live in one man. A daughter learns what steadiness feels like at home. If those children are still years away, let the fear of the Lord begin in you now.

A just walk blesses the children who come after it.

“The just man walketh in his integrity: his children are blessed after him.”

Proverbs 20:7

Children are blessed after a father who tells the truth when it costs him, comes home when he said he would, and serves Christ in the kitchen the same way he speaks of Christ among the brothers. They are watching the walk.

A psalm of blessing names the wife and the children in the house.

“Thy wife shall be as a fruitful vine by the sides of thine house: thy children like olive plants round about thy table. Behold, that thus shall the man be blessed that feareth the LORD.”

Psalm 128:3–4

A fruitful vine by the sides of the house is a wife who is living and near, with her husband beside her. Olive plants round about the table are children close enough to be known. Ask your wife what she has been carrying, and stay until you understand it.

“Husbands, love your wives, and be not bitter against them.”

Colossians 3:19

A shared roof still feels tight when a husband’s tone is bitter. Love puts the sharpness down, so that she meets a partner when she meets you. The same nearness becomes a refuge for children when they are safe with their father.

“Fathers, provoke not your children to anger, lest they be discouraged.”

Colossians 3:21

Mocking and comparing provoke a child and leave him discouraged. Correct him, keep your voice low, and stay close enough that discipline feels like care.

God told fathers to pass His truth on, so the next generation would set its hope in Him.

“For he established a testimony in Jacob, and appointed a law in Israel, which he commanded our fathers, that they should make them known to their children: That the generation to come might know them, even the children which should be born; who should arise and declare them to their children: That they might set their hope in God, and not forget the works of God, but keep his commandments:”

Psalm 78:5–7

Hope in God takes root when someone at home makes His works known. If you have no children yet, learn that truth now, so you will have words ready when God gives you a house.

Joshua settled the choice for his own roof.

“And if it seem evil unto you to serve the LORD, choose you this day whom ye will serve; whether the gods which your fathers served that were on the other side of the flood, or the gods of the Amorites, in whose land ye dwell: but as for me and my house, we will serve the LORD.”

Joshua 24:15

A home with a present father hears whom it serves. You are not the savior of the house. Jesus is. Your nearness is one way your family learns that God comes close.

A way to begin this week

  1. At one meal, name a work of God out loud, where your household can hear it.
  2. After you correct a child, stay until the relationship is warm again.
  3. Ask your wife what would help her this week, and do that before you offer a plan of your own.
  4. If you are not yet a husband or a father, keep one habit of integrity, and pray that God would make you a refuge.
  5. Once at the table, say in your own words that this house will serve the Lord.

A prayer

Father, thank You for the wife and children You have given, and for the home You may yet give me to lead. Make me a man who fears You. Let my children find a refuge, and let my wife flourish because I am near and free of bitterness. Open my mouth about Your works, and let this house serve You. Forgive me where I have been absent, harsh, or silent. In Jesus’ name, amen.

Who is my one?

“What man of you, having an hundred sheep, if he lose one of them, doth not leave the ninety and nine in the wilderness, and go after that which is lost, until he find it? And when he hath found it, he layeth it on his shoulders, rejoicing. And when he cometh home, he calleth together his friends and neighbours, saying unto them, Rejoice with me; for I have found my sheep which was lost. I say unto you, that likewise joy shall be in heaven over one sinner that repenteth, more than over ninety and nine just persons, which need no repentance.”

Luke 15:4–7
