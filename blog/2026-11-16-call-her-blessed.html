Call Her Blessed

Proverbs 31:28 calls a husband to praise his wife where she can hear it, and Numbers 6:24–26 is a blessing a present man can pray over his children. When his mouth gives life, his wife is honored, his children are named with hope, and the home takes a gentler tone.

Men, a wife can live under a faithful roof and still go a long time without hearing that she is a gift. A child can be corrected often and blessed rarely. This word is for husbands, for fathers, and for the man who will be one. Scripture in this post is quoted from the King James Version.

“Her children arise up, and call her blessed; her husband also, and he praiseth her. Many daughters have done virtuously, but thou excellest them all.”

Proverbs 31:28–29

The children call her blessed, and her husband praises her. He tells her she excels, and he says it where she can hear it.

“Death and life are in the power of the tongue: and they that love it shall eat the fruit thereof.”

Proverbs 18:21

A cutting remark, a joke at her expense, and silence where thanks belonged are a kind of death. A true word of honor is life.

Praise her where she can hear it

If the Lord has given you a wife, name what is real: the faith she keeps on a heavy week, the patience you have not earned, the work nobody claps for. Say it at the table. A thought that stays in your head is not praise she can live on.

Paul names the word that may leave a saved mouth.

“Let no corrupt communication proceed out of your mouth, but that which is good to the use of edifying, that it may minister grace unto the hearers.”

Ephesians 4:29

Corrupt speech tears down. A word that ministers grace builds the hearer. Tell the truth about a hard day, and set a true word beside it.

If you are not married yet, thank one person near you in a full sentence. The mouth you train now is the mouth a wife would live with. “Husbands, love your wives, and be not bitter against them” (Colossians 3:19). If you have spoken against her, repair it before you praise her.

A battle you fight beside her

Husbands and wives fight a real battle with the tongue. One sharp evening can arm you both. Ask, in a calm hour, which words have wounded her, and hear her. Then speak the true good you see before the week ends. If she answers sharply, give a soft and true word anyway.

Bless the children in your arms

“And he took them up in his arms, put his hands upon them, and blessed them.”

Mark 10:16

His disciples “rebuked those that brought them” (Mark 10:13). “But when Jesus saw it, he was much displeased, and said unto them, Suffer the little children to come unto me, and forbid them not: for of such is the kingdom of God” (Mark 10:14). If God has given you children, bless them yourself. Hold the small one. Set a hand on the shoulder of the one nearly grown, and speak a true word over him.

“The LORD bless thee, and keep thee: The LORD make his face shine upon thee, and be gracious unto thee: The LORD lift up his countenance upon thee, and give thee peace.”

Numbers 6:24–26

You do not stand in Aaron’s office. Pray these words over your house, and let them hear you. A face turned toward a child gives peace a place to land. A face in a phone is turned away. Be present long enough to look at them.

Esau said, “Hast thou but one blessing, my father? bless me, even me also, O my father.” And Esau lifted up his voice, and wept (Genesis 27:38). Bless the easy child and the hard one. Do not let failure be the only name you speak. If children are still ahead, pray Numbers 6:24–26 now.

What the home hears

A praised wife can rest. A son learns to honor his mother from a father who does it. A daughter learns the sound of a man who gives life. You cannot praise what you have not noticed, so sit long enough to see one true thing, and say it while the hour is warm.

The one

“What man of you, having an hundred sheep, if he lose one of them, doth not leave the ninety and nine in the wilderness, and go after that which is lost, until he find it?” (Luke 15:4). And “joy shall be in heaven over one sinner that repenteth” (Luke 15:7). Who is my one? Your wife, a child who hears little besides correction, or one man spoken of in criticism and not in prayer. You cannot save him. Christ does. Speak a true word, pray a blessing, and go.

Three things to do this week

  1. Today, praise your wife for one true thing, where she can hear you. If you are not married, thank one person God has set near you.
  2. This week, bless one child by name and pray Numbers 6:24–26 aloud. If no child is in the house, pray it for someone young near you, or for children you are asking the Lord to give.
  3. Name your one. Speak well of that person, pray, and take one step before the week ends.

Who is my one? Begin with the person under your roof who needs a true word. Then ask the Lord for one soul beyond your door. Read the mission, wear the Reach One bracelet as a reminder to reach one soul, or give to the work.

A prayer

Father, death and life are in the power of the tongue, and I have spent too many words on what is wrong. Forgive me. Teach me to praise the wife You have given me, and to keep a mouth ready to bless her if that gift is still ahead. Help me take the children up and bless them, as the Lord Jesus did. Make Your face shine on this house, and give it peace. Send me after the one. In Jesus’ name, amen.
