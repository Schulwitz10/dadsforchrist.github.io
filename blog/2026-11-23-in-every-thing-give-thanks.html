In Every Thing Give Thanks

First Thessalonians 5:18 calls a man to give thanks in every thing, for this is the will of God in Christ Jesus concerning him. When he leads his household in gratitude through a hard season, his wife hears thanks beside her in the battle, the children learn thankfulness by watching and hearing him thank God out loud, and one man who may be alone is not forgotten.

Men, this is Thanksgiving week. Gratitude is more than a holiday meal. It is daily leadership. A man gives thanks to God when the week is bright and when it is not. This word is for husbands, for fathers, and for the man who will be one. Scripture in this post is quoted from the King James Version.

“In every thing give thanks: for this is the will of God in Christ Jesus concerning you.”

1 Thessalonians 5:18

In every thing. The verse does not wait for an easy season. Thanks is the will of God in Christ Jesus, and a Thursday feast does not cover a closed mouth the rest of the week.

“Enter into his gates with thanksgiving, and into his courts with praise: be thankful unto him, and bless his name” (Psalm 100:4). Enter with thanks, and bless His name where people can hear it.

“Be careful for nothing; but in every thing by prayer and supplication with thanksgiving let your requests be made known unto God. And the peace of God, which passeth all understanding, shall keep your hearts and minds through Christ Jesus.”

Philippians 4:6–7

Bring the request and the thanks together. His peace keeps heart and mind through Christ Jesus while the trouble remains.

What she receives

A wife fights battles that do not pause for a holiday. Money, sickness, a struggling child, grief at supper. You can share a roof and still thank God only where she never hears it. Give thanks beside her, in the middle of the fight, not only after you have called it finished.

If the week has been sharp, say so, then thank God for her where she can hear it. Thanks does not pretend the battle is small. It stands with her and confesses that God is still good. If you are not married yet, thank God out loud now.

What the children learn

Children learn thankfulness by watching and by hearing. A sigh teaches them that life is a complaint. Thanks spoken to God teaches them that He is worthy of praise in an ordinary house.

“And these words, which I command thee this day, shall be in thine heart: And thou shalt teach them diligently unto thy children, and shalt talk of them when thou sittest in thine house, and when thou walkest by the way, and when thou liest down, and when thou risest up.”

Deuteronomy 6:6–7

Put the words in your own heart first. Then talk of them when you sit, walk, lie down, and rise. Thank God there, not only in a speech saved for the holiday.

“Be ye thankful,” and do all “in the name of the Lord Jesus, giving thanks to God and the Father by him” (Colossians 3:15–17). Let the word of Christ dwell in the house, and give thanks while you teach. If no child is listening yet, thank God as a man who will be heard.

A present man at the table

A present man at the table prays and names God’s goodness before anyone eats. Put the phone down. Thank Him for the food, then name one mercy that is not the food. Specific thanks teaches a house what to look for.

“And one of them, when he saw that he was healed, turned back, and with a loud voice glorified God, And fell down on his face at his feet, giving him thanks: and he was a Samaritan. And Jesus answering said, Were there not ten cleansed? but where are the nine?”

Luke 17:15–17

Ten were cleansed. One turned back, and Jesus asked where the nine were. Be the one who returns, and glorify God where your household can hear it. If you live alone, speak the thanks anyway. Your heart needs the practice of turning back.

Who is my one?

Jesus told of a man who leaves the ninety and nine and goes after one lost sheep “until he find it.” Joy in heaven is “over one sinner that repenteth” (Luke 15:4–7). Think of one man who may be alone this week, or struggling and quiet about it. He may have no table, or a table where no one prays.

Pray for him by name, then reach him. Invite him to a meal, take a plate to his door, or call and stay long enough to hear the truth. Thank God in front of him.

Who is my one? Lead the people under your roof in thanks. Then reach one man who may be sitting this week without it. Read the mission, wear the Reach One bracelet as a reminder to reach one soul, or give to the work.

Give thanks this week

  1. Thank God in one hard thing, out loud. Name the battle honestly, and thank Him in it. If you have a wife, do it where she can hear, and ask what she is thankful for while the fight is still on.
  2. Pray at the table and name one goodness of God that is not the food. Put the phone away until the prayer is finished. If you eat alone, pray the same way before the first bite.
  3. Let them hear it. If you have children, thank God in their hearing once each day, and ask each child to name one mercy.
  4. Reach one man. Pray for him by name, then invite him to a meal or call him this week. Stay long enough to listen.

A prayer

Father, teach me to give thanks in every thing, for this is Your will in Christ Jesus. Forgive the days I walked on with the nine. Help me thank You beside my wife, if You have given me one, in the battles we face together. If You have given me children, let them learn thankfulness by hearing their father bless Your name. Make me present at the table, and give me one man to reach. In Jesus’ name, amen.
