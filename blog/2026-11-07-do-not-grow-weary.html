Do Not Grow Weary

The long work of a husband and a present father can make a man want to quit. Galatians 6:9 calls him to keep doing good at home, and a wife, a child, and a house are steadied when he does not give up.

Men, the good work of a home is often quiet and long. You come in after a hard day, and the house still needs you present. The marriage has gone quiet with distance. A child pushes back at the line you held yesterday. Battles a husband and wife walk through together can make a man want to stop leading. He may still bring a paycheck home and still quit the good only he can do: staying, speaking gently, praying, and beginning again. This word is for husbands, for fathers, and for the man who will be one. If you lay that faithfulness down, the people closest to you live with the absence.

Paul names the temptation.

“And let us not be weary in well doing: for in due season we shall reap, if we faint not.”

Galatians 6:9

Do not grow weary of doing good. Well doing at home is the next kind word when you are spent, a seat at the table instead of a screen, and a correction that stays when a child resists. Weariness says none of it is working. Paul answers with a harvest. In due season we shall reap, if we faint not. The season is God’s. Fainting is quitting the good before that season comes.

Your labor is not wasted.

“Therefore, my beloved brethren, be ye stedfast, unmoveable, always abounding in the work of the Lord, forasmuch as ye know that your labour is not in vain in the Lord.”

1 Corinthians 15:58

Be steadfast and unmovable, always abounding in the work of the Lord. A quiet marriage can feel vain, and a child who pushes back can make a man feel useless. God does not weigh a home by one evening. If you are not a husband or a father yet, practice this unseen work now. A man who quits small faithfulness will not become steady on the day a family needs him.

Look to Jesus, so you do not faint.

“Looking unto Jesus the author and finisher of our faith; who for the joy that was set before him endured the cross, despising the shame, and is set down at the right hand of the throne of God. For consider him that endured such contradiction of sinners against himself, lest ye be wearied and faint in your minds.”

Hebrews 12:2–3

The race often runs through a man’s own house. Lay aside resentment, the score you keep with your wife, and the habit of withdrawing when the rooms get hard. For the joy set before him, Jesus endured the cross. Consider him, so you are not wearied in your mind. You are not asked to manufacture endurance. Keep your eyes on Christ and take the next faithful step.

What a home receives when a man stays

A wife can tell a finished man from a faithful one. Money, grief, and a long stretch without warmth are real, and they can make you both wonder if trying is worth it. If you quit leading, she carries the spiritual weight of the house alone. When you do not give up, she has a partner who comes back after the hard day. She can hope, and she can tell the truth. Your steadiness tells her she is not standing in the marriage by herself.

Children learn perseverance by watching it. A son who sees you show up tired learns that a man keeps his place. A daughter learns that love does not leave because the evening went badly. When they push back and you stay calm, they learn that correction and affection can live in one man. If you give up, they learn that pressure wins. A father who does not faint shows them the God who does not abandon His people.

The house takes its shape from that perseverance. Apologies still happen. Scripture is still opened. The worst hour of the week does not set the temperature of every room. Paul adds, “As we have therefore opportunity, let us do good unto all men, especially unto them who are of the household of faith” (Galatians 6:10). That household begins at your table.

If God has not given you a wife or children yet, do not wait. Keep your word when you are tired. Pray when prayer feels dry. Do not faint before a home you cannot yet see.

Three things to do this week

  1. After the hardest hour of one day, do one good thing before you withdraw. Ask your wife how she is, sit with your child, or read a short passage aloud. If you live alone, do that good for one person near you.
  2. Tell your wife you are not quitting one battle you are walking through, and pray with her that you will not grow weary of doing good. If you are not married, pray that sentence for your own heart.
  3. When a child or someone in your care pushes back, stay. Keep your voice low, hold the boundary once, and follow it with one ordinary kindness. If you have no child yet, practice that patience with someone who tests you.

Who is my one? Begin with the person under your roof who needs you to keep doing good. Then ask the Lord for one soul outside your door. Read the mission, wear the Reach One bracelet as a reminder to reach one soul, or give to the work.

A prayer

Father, You see the days when I want to stop. Forgive me for growing weary of doing good. Make me steadfast, and teach me to look to Jesus, who endured the cross and did not faint. Help me come home present after a hard day, stay gentle in a quiet season, and keep loving when I am pushed back. Guard my wife and the children You give me. If that family is still ahead, keep me faithful now. In Jesus’ name, amen.
