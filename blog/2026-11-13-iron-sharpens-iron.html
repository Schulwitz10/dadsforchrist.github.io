Iron Sharpens Iron

A man was never meant to fight for his marriage, his children, and his faith alone. Proverbs 27:17 calls him to walk with godly men who sharpen, confess, and pray, so he comes home more present and steadier, and goes after the one man who has drifted.

Men, a man was never meant to fight for his marriage, his children, and his faith alone. Many of us still try, and we call the silence strength. This word is for husbands, for fathers, and for the man who will be one. Scripture in this post is quoted from the King James Version.

“Iron sharpeneth iron; so a man sharpeneth the countenance of his friend.”

Proverbs 27:17

Iron does not sharpen itself. A friend who will tell you the truth, and stay while you hear it, puts the edge back and leaves you with a clearer face.

Why a man goes alone

Isolation looks like a full week and sounds like “I can handle it.” Pride hates being seen in need. Shame does not want a godly man to hear the anger, the habit, or the drift. Life does not settle while you wait. You can fill a pew and a workday and still have no man who knows your soul.

What a closed life costs

If the Lord has given you a wife, she feels it first. She carries what you will not name, or she lives with a husband who has gone quiet. She was not meant to be the only one who knows whether you are standing. She is left to guess.

Children feel a father who is in the room and still gone. A son learns to hide his battles and call it strength. A daughter learns that nearness is not the same as being known. Hidden sin stays hidden, and the house takes the temper of a man no one can lift.

If a wife and children are still ahead of you, open your life to godly men now.

Walk with men who sharpen

“Two are better than one; because they have a good reward for their labour. For if they fall, the one will lift up his fellow: but woe to him that is alone when he falleth; for he hath not another to help him up.”

Ecclesiastes 4:9–10

Walk with a few godly men. When you fall in patience, purity, or faith, one of them can help you up. Woe is the word for the man with no such fellow.

“Confess your faults one to another, and pray one for another, that ye may be healed. The effectual fervent prayer of a righteous man availeth much.”

James 5:16

Name the fault. Do not bury it in “I have been struggling.” Pray for one another by name.

“And let us consider one another to provoke unto love and to good works: Not forsaking the assembling of ourselves together, as the manner of some is; but exhorting one another: and so much the more, as ye see the day approaching.”

Hebrews 10:24–25

A godly friend stirs you toward love at home and toward good that a child can see. Keep meeting. Do not make a manner of staying away.

“Bear ye one another’s burdens, and so fulfil the law of Christ.”

Galatians 6:2

To bear a burden is to stay. Ask next month whether the fault still has him, and let him ask you.

What he brings home

A sharpened man comes home more present, because someone has already asked where his mind has been. He is steadier, because the hard thing was spoken in the light before supper. His wife is not left as the only keeper of his soul. His children receive a father who can repent. The house can tell he is being kept.

If you are not yet a husband or a father, let men sharpen you now. The face you bring home later is being formed now.

Be the friend who goes

Then be the friend who goes. Jesus goes after the one sheep still in the wilderness.

“What man of you, having an hundred sheep, if he lose one of them, doth not leave the ninety and nine in the wilderness, and go after that which is lost, until he find it? And when he hath found it, he layeth it on his shoulders, rejoicing. And when he cometh home, he calleth together his friends and neighbours, saying unto them, Rejoice with me; for I have found my sheep which was lost. I say unto you, that likewise joy shall be in heaven over one sinner that repenteth, more than over ninety and nine just persons, which need no repentance.”

Luke 15:4–7

You cannot save him. Christ does. Go, sit down, ask the true question, and pray before you leave. Be the friend who will not let the silence win.

Three things to do this week

  1. Name how you isolate, whether pride, shame, or a full calendar. Tell the Lord today, and tell one godly man before the week ends.
  2. Give him one hour. Read Proverbs 27:17 together. Confess one real fault, pray for each other by name, and set the next meeting before you part.
  3. Ask, Who is my one? Name one man who has drifted, and go after him this week with a call, a visit, or a meal.

A dull man makes a heavier house. Let a godly man sharpen you, then be that iron for a man who has slipped.

Who is my one? Be the friend who goes after one man who has drifted. Read the mission, wear the Reach One bracelet as a reminder to reach one soul, or give to the work.

A prayer

Father, You never meant me to fight alone for my faith, my marriage, or my children. Forgive me for hiding. Give me men who will sharpen my countenance, hear my confession, and pray. Make me present and steady at home. Send me after the one who has drifted. In Jesus’ name, amen.
