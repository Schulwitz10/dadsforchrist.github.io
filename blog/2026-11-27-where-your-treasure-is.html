Where Your Treasure Is

Matthew 6:21 says that where your treasure is, there will your heart be also. On the day after Thanksgiving, while the world chases things to buy, a man’s family needs his presence more than his presents: children remember time and attention, a husband and wife fight money’s pressure side by side, faith is invested through prayer and Scripture, and time is given to one man who is struggling or alone.

Men, yesterday was Thanksgiving. Today the world spends itself chasing things to buy. A man can come home with full bags and still have been absent. Your family needs your presence more than your presents. This word is for husbands, for fathers, and for the man who will be one. Scripture in this post is quoted from the King James Version.

“Lay not up for yourselves treasures upon earth, where moth and rust doth corrupt, and where thieves break through and steal: But lay up for yourselves treasures in heaven, where neither moth nor rust doth corrupt, and where thieves do not break through nor steal: For where your treasure is, there will your heart be also.”

Matthew 6:19–21

He tells you where your heart will follow. Hunt the next purchase, and your heart is in the cart. Give the day to the people God has given you, and your heart is in the house.

What the children remember

A child does not keep a catalog of what came in a bag. What stays is time and attention. A son remembers whether his father looked up. A daughter remembers whether he stayed for the story. A package can be forgotten by evening. A man who stays cannot be replaced by one.

Sit down, and do not hurry them. When a man is present, children learn that they matter more than the next thing to buy, and that faith is a man in the room rather than a speech from the doorway. If no child is in your house yet, practice that presence now, so it is already your way when children come.

Fight the money battle together

Money is one of the battles a husband and wife face together. A thin month or a long list can turn them into opponents. Do not fight her. Stand beside her and fight the pressure.

“Better is little with the fear of the LORD than great treasure and trouble therewith.”

Proverbs 15:16

Paul wrote the same contentment to Timothy.

“But godliness with contentment is great gain. For we brought nothing into this world, and it is certain we can carry nothing out. And having food and raiment let us be therewith content.”

1 Timothy 6:6–8

The letter to the Hebrews sets that contentment on a promise.

“Let your conversation be without covetousness; and be content with such things as ye have: for he hath said, I will never leave thee, nor forsake thee.”

Hebrews 13:5

In this verse, conversation means the way you live. Be content, so that pressure does not turn you against her. Sit with her, name what you can spend, and pray over that number before either of you buys. If you are not married yet, set the limit before the Lord and keep it. The fight is against a heart that wants more than God has given.

Lay up treasure in heaven

For a husband and a father, laying up treasure in heaven comes home. Invest in your wife’s faith and in your children’s faith. Pray with them. Open the Scripture where they can hear your voice. Be in the room. A prayer at the table, a verse before bed, and staying while your wife tells you what she is carrying are treasures moth and rust cannot touch. If you have no wife and no children yet, begin the same way with the people God has already set near you.

Who is my one?

The richest gift this season may not be anything you can wrap. It may be time given to one man who is struggling or alone.

“What man of you, having an hundred sheep, if he lose one of them, doth not leave the ninety and nine in the wilderness, and go after that which is lost, until he find it? And when he hath found it, he layeth it on his shoulders, rejoicing. And when he cometh home, he calleth together his friends and neighbours, saying unto them, Rejoice with me; for I have found my sheep which was lost. I say unto you, that likewise joy shall be in heaven over one sinner that repenteth, more than over ninety and nine just persons, which need no repentance.”

Luke 15:4–7

Some men will spend this weekend in a quiet house, or under a weight they have not said aloud. The Shepherd still goes after the one until he finds it.

Who is my one? Name him before the weekend slips away. Call him, or sit with him if you can. If you are that man, you are not forgotten. Be present where you are, then reach one other man who is struggling. Read the mission, wear the Reach One bracelet as a reminder to reach one soul, or give to the work.

Before this weekend is over

  1. If God has given you a wife, set a spending limit together, then pray over it before you buy. If you are not married, set the limit before the Lord and keep it.
  2. Give each child an unhurried hour this weekend. If you have no children yet, give that hour to someone who needs your full attention.
  3. Put the phone away at the table. Let the people in the room have your face.
  4. Call one man who is struggling or alone. Do not wait for a quieter week.

A prayer

Father, You see where my treasure has been. Forgive me for chasing what moth and rust will corrupt, and for handing my family presents when they needed my presence. Teach me to be content, and to stand with my wife in the money fight instead of against her. Help me lay up treasure in heaven by praying, by opening Your Word, and by being in the room. Show me one man who is struggling or alone, and give me courage to give him my time. In Jesus’ name, amen.
