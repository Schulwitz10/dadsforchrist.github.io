Redeeming the Time

Ephesians 5:15–16 calls a man to walk circumspectly and redeem the time, because the days are evil, and Psalm 90:12 asks God to teach him to number his days. The hours with his wife and children are a stewardship to spend now, with the phone down, at the table, in bedtime prayer, and listening, instead of handing the house only what a busy day leaves over.

Men, a full day can still leave the people you love with what remains. Work takes the first hours. The house gets whatever is left. This word is for husbands, for fathers, and for the man who will be one. Scripture in this post is quoted from the King James Version.

Paul told the church how to walk.

“See then that ye walk circumspectly, not as fools, but as wise, Redeeming the time, because the days are evil.”

Ephesians 5:15–16

Watch where you step. Redeeming the time means buying the hour back. The days are evil, and they will take what you do not purpose to give. The hour in front of you is the one you can still spend. Someday is not a day you can find.

Moses prayed that God would teach His people to count.

“So teach us to number our days, that we may apply our hearts unto wisdom.”

Psalm 90:12

You do not know how many suppers are left, or how many bedtimes, or how many evenings your wife will still hope you sit and hear her. Apply your heart to the days you have.

“Whereas ye know not what shall be on the morrow. For what is your life? It is even a vapour, that appeareth for a little time, and then vanisheth away” (James 4:14). A child at the table will not wait for a quieter week. Neither will a wife who has carried the evening alone.

The hours are a stewardship

Time with your wife and your children is a trust. You can provide and still be gone. You can mean well and still hand them the tired edge of the day. A steward answers for the hours in his hands.

A busy man gives the family what the job did not use, and he calls that love. The work may be real. An empty chair is real too. Leftover time teaches a house that they come after everything else.

If you are not a husband yet, the habit is forming now. A man who will not put the phone down will not suddenly do it when a wife is waiting.

What presence does in a house

Presence is the phone down while you are with them, a seat at the table, a few sentences of prayer at bedtime, and a listening ear until your wife is finished.

Moses tied God’s words to those ordinary hours.

“And these words, which I command thee this day, shall be in thine heart: And thou shalt teach them diligently unto thy children, and shalt talk of them when thou sittest in thine house, and when thou walkest by the way, and when thou liest down, and when thou risest up.”

Deuteronomy 6:6–7

A son watches whether his father looks up. A daughter learns whether she is worth the interruption. If no child is in the house yet, keep the words in your heart now, so the talk is ready when God gives you someone to teach.

A wife is steadied when you listen and when you pray before sleep. She is not left as the only one holding the day. A home takes its tone from the man who is truly in it.

The hour you still have

The days are evil, and they fill themselves. Put the phone down tonight. Come to the table. Ask what the day cost her, and stay until you have heard it.

The one at your own table

Jesus would not leave the one sheep lost.

“What man of you, having an hundred sheep, if he lose one of them, doth not leave the ninety and nine in the wilderness, and go after that which is lost, until he find it? And when he hath found it, he layeth it on his shoulders, rejoicing. And when he cometh home, he calleth together his friends and neighbours, saying unto them, Rejoice with me; for I have found my sheep which was lost. I say unto you, that likewise joy shall be in heaven over one sinner that repenteth, more than over ninety and nine just persons, which need no repentance.”

Luke 15:4–7

Who is my one? The first one may be at your own table: a wife who no longer expects you, or a child who has stopped asking you to stay. Go after that one before you look for a larger work. You cannot save that one. Christ does. Being present is how a man goes.

Three things to do this week

  1. Tonight, put the phone down for one meal. Sit through it.
  2. Before the week ends, listen to your wife, or to one person near you, until that person is finished. Pray a few plain sentences before you sleep. If a child is in the house, pray at bedtime with that child.
  3. Name one hour you have been saving for someday, and give that hour to your house this week.

Who is my one? Begin with the person at your table who has been living on leftover time. Then ask the Lord for one soul past your door. Read the mission, wear the Reach One bracelet as a reminder to reach one soul, or give to the work.

A prayer

Father, teach us to number our days, that we may apply our hearts unto wisdom. Forgive the hours I gave this house only what was left. Make me wise. Help me redeem the time still in front of me. Set the phone down, put me at the table, and give me a listening ear for the wife You have given or will give. Let Your words be in this home when we sit, when we walk, and when we lie down. Send me first after the one at my own table. In Jesus’ name, amen.
