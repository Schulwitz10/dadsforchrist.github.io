Turn the Hearts

Malachi 4:6 and Luke 1:17 call for a father's heart to turn toward his children. When that turning is real, a child is known, a wife is no longer alone in the raising, and a home is made ready for the Lord.

Men, a house can be full and a father’s heart can still be somewhere else. He may pay what is owed and still keep his face turned from the children. This word is for husbands, for fathers, and for the man who will be one. If children are in your care, they live with the direction of your heart. If they are still ahead of you, God can turn that heart now. Scripture in this post is quoted from the King James Version.

The Old Testament closes with a promise and a warning.

“And he shall turn the heart of the fathers to the children, and the heart of the children to their fathers, lest I come and smite the earth with a curse.”

Malachi 4:6

God does the turning. Living as a turned man is your part. A heart that stays turned away leaves a generation unfaced, and Malachi sets a curse beside that refusal. Ask Him to turn you, and then stay turned on an ordinary day.

The Gospel opens on the same promise. An angel says John will go before the Lord.

“And he shall go before him in the spirit and power of Elias, to turn the hearts of the fathers to the children, and the disobedient to the wisdom of the just; to make ready a people prepared for the Lord.”

Luke 1:17

John comes in the spirit and power of Elijah so a people can be made ready for the Lord. That readiness includes a man whose heart faces his children. Faith that never turns toward the souls under your roof is not what Luke describes.

What the children receive

A turned heart shows up as presence. A child needs a man who looks up, learns the name of the fear, and stays after the correction. The Lord is the pattern of that pity.

“Like as a father pitieth his children, so the LORD pitieth them that fear him.”

Psalm 103:13

He feels for those who fear Him, and He moves toward them. A turned heart does not excuse sin, and it does not stay far off while a child carries it. A son learns that strength can come close. A daughter learns that a man’s attention can be safe. That presence has to outlast one good evening.

“The just man walketh in his integrity: his children are blessed after him.”

Proverbs 20:7

Children are blessed after a man who keeps the promise, gives the apology, and prays where they can hear it.

“And, ye fathers, provoke not your children to wrath: but bring them up in the nurture and admonition of the Lord.”

Ephesians 6:4

Nurture feeds a soul. Admonition tells the truth. A sharp tongue, a shaming joke, or days of coldness can provoke wrath. A turned heart corrects and stays. It opens the Scripture, and it lets a child see that his father needs Jesus too.

If you have no children yet, ask the Lord to turn you now toward the people He will give you.

What a wife receives

A wife can tell when her husband is in the chair and gone. She has often held the child’s fear, the quarrel that never finished, and the question nobody answered. When his heart turns toward the children, she is no longer the only one present. She gains a partner.

Ask what she has been carrying, and take a real share of it. Malachi’s word runs both ways, fathers toward children and children toward fathers (Malachi 4:6). She is not left to hold the generations together alone.

What the home becomes

People are known at the table. Correction has warmth after it. Prayer is normal. The house learns whom it answers to, because a man has brought his heart back under the roof and under the Lord.

Where the turning is refused, Malachi names a curse (Malachi 4:6). The bond thins, and a wife carries what two were meant to carry. Luke names the hope beside that warning: this turning helps make ready a people prepared for the Lord (Luke 1:17). Christ saves. A turned heart points a wife, a child, and a home toward Him.

Three things to do this week

  1. Each morning, pray by name for one child, or for the child you hope the Lord will give. If a child is in the house, name one true thing you see and are glad for.
  2. Leave the phone in another room for one meal or one bedtime. Hear the day out. If you have a wife, take one weight she has been carrying for the children.
  3. Before you sleep, say so if you spoke harshly or were home with your heart elsewhere. Read a short passage with whoever will listen, and ask the Lord to turn your heart.

A paycheck can keep a roof. A turned heart keeps a child from growing up unknown, gives a wife a partner, and teaches a home that the Lord is near. Ask for the turning Malachi promised and Luke announced, and live it in the next ordinary hour.

Who is my one? Begin with the child the Lord is calling you to face, and with your wife if He has given you one. Then ask Him for one soul beyond your door. Read the mission, wear the Reach One bracelet as a reminder to reach one soul, or give to the work.

A prayer

Father, thank You for the promise that You turn the hearts of fathers toward their children. Forgive me where my heart has been under the roof and still turned away. Give me the pity You have for those who fear You. Teach me to bring them up in the nurture and admonition of the Lord, and to stand with their mother in that work. Make this home ready for You. In Jesus’ name, amen.
