Round About Thy Table

Psalm 128:1–4 calls the man who fears the LORD and walks in His ways blessed, with his wife as a fruitful vine and his children like olive plants round about his table. This Thanksgiving week, a man is needed at that table and not only in the house: phone down, leading grace and conversation, so his wife does not carry the table alone, his children are tended where they grow, and one man who may be alone is invited in.

Men, this is Thanksgiving week. A man can be in the house for the feast and never take his chair. This word is for husbands, for fathers, and for the man who will be one. Scripture in this post is quoted from the King James Version.

“Blessed is every one that feareth the LORD; that walketh in his ways. For thou shalt eat the labour of thine hands: happy shalt thou be, and it shall be well with thee. Thy wife shall be as a fruitful vine by the sides of thine house: thy children like olive plants round about thy table. Behold, that thus shall the man be blessed that feareth the LORD.”

Psalm 128:1–4

Fear of the LORD and a walk in His ways come first. Then the psalm sets that man at the table, with his wife as a vine by the sides of the house and his children like olive plants round about him. The blessing is a man who is present, not a man who only passes through.

You can live at the address and miss the table. One more message, one more scroll, a plate carried to another room. Your family knows you came home, and they know the chair stayed empty. Put the phone down before you sit. Lead the grace. Stay for the conversation.

What she receives

A wife can set every dish and still carry the table alone if you only visit the hour. Sit and stay. Lead the grace so the blessing is not left to her, and ask about her day until you have heard the answer. The vine is by the sides of the house on purpose. Thank God for her where she can hear her name, and take one ordinary weight so she can eat while the food is hot. If you are not married yet, practice presence now.

What the children learn

Olive plants grow where they are tended. They do not become strong because a man is somewhere in the house. They grow around the table where he sits, and they learn by watching him: the phone put away or still in his hand, grace spoken or skipped, their story held or hurried past.

“And these words, which I command thee this day, shall be in thine heart: And thou shalt teach them diligently unto thy children, and shalt talk of them when thou sittest in thine house, and when thou walkest by the way, and when thou liest down, and when thou risest up.”

Deuteronomy 6:6–7

Put these words in your heart first, then talk of them when you sit in your house. Read one verse and name one way you are trying to walk in it. If no child is at your table yet, keep the habit. You are already becoming the man they will watch.

What the home becomes

A home takes its tone from the table where its people gather. Before anyone eats, thank the Lord for the food, the faces there, and Jesus Christ. Then ask what was heavy and what was good. A God-fearing man at that table does more for a house than a fine speech from another room. If the week has been sharp, sit anyway.

Who is my one?

“What man of you, having an hundred sheep, if he lose one of them, doth not leave the ninety and nine in the wilderness, and go after that which is lost, until he find it? And when he hath found it, he layeth it on his shoulders, rejoicing. And when he cometh home, he calleth together his friends and neighbours, saying unto them, Rejoice with me; for I have found my sheep which was lost. I say unto you, that likewise joy shall be in heaven over one sinner that repenteth, more than over ninety and nine just persons, which need no repentance.”

Luke 15:4–7

Some men have no table this week. Some are under a roof and still eat alone.

Who is my one? Name him before the Lord. Invite him to your table, or carry a meal to his door and sit with him. If you are the man who may be alone this holiday, the Shepherd has not lost sight of you. Be present with the people under your roof, then reach one man who may have nowhere to sit. Sit down this week

  1. Put the phone in another room, and lead a short grace before anyone eats.
  2. If God has given you a wife, ask what she carried today, thank Him for her where she can hear it, and take one job so she is not holding the meal alone.
  3. If you have children, ask each one a real question and talk of the Lord’s words while you sit. If you do not yet, give that attention to the person across from you.
  4. Pray for one man by name, then invite him to the table or take a plate and stay until you have listened.

A prayer

Father, make me a man who fears You and walks in Your ways. Forgive the nights I was in the house and missing from the table. Help me put the phone down, lead grace, and stay. If You have given me a wife, let her not carry the table alone. If You have given me children, tend them as olive plants round about a table where I sit and speak of You. Show me one man who may be alone, and give me courage to invite him. In Jesus’ name, amen.
