Two Are Better Than One

A husband and wife face stress, money, illness, conflict, and spiritual attack together. Ecclesiastes 4:9–12 calls a man to stand with his wife, with Christ as the third strand, so his wife, his children, and his home are held.

Men, a hard week shows whether a husband stands with his wife or turns against her. Stress fills a house. Money runs thin. Illness stays. Under those troubles is a spiritual fight. This word is for husbands, for fathers, and for the man who will be one. Scripture in this post is quoted from the King James Version.

Solomon named what a lone man forgets.

“Two are better than one; because they have a good reward for their labour. For if they fall, the one will lift up his fellow: but woe to him that is alone when he falleth; for he hath not another to help him up. Again, if two lie together, then they have heat: but how can one be warm alone? And if one prevail against him, two shall withstand him; and a threefold cord is not quickly broken.”

Ecclesiastes 4:9–12

Solomon does not name the third strand. Do not leave it blank. The two are a man and his wife. The third is the Lord. Two strands can fray. A cord with Christ in it is not quickly broken.

God joined a husband and wife before the battle arrived.

“Therefore shall a man leave his father and his mother, and shall cleave unto his wife: and they shall be one flesh.”

Genesis 2:24

Leave, and cleave. One flesh is a life you keep choosing. Take her side and stay there. Do not fight on as a man alone, and do not make her the opponent.

Battles that belong to both of you

Name them. Stress makes a man short, then silent. A thin month can turn supper into a trial. Illness can wear out the one who steadies the house. A conflict can leave two people who have forgotten they are one flesh.

Do not mistake her for the fight.

“For we wrestle not against flesh and blood, but against principalities, against powers, against the rulers of the darkness of this world, against spiritual wickedness in high places.”

Ephesians 6:12

Your wife is not the enemy. A bill is not the enemy, and neither is a sickness. Vanishing into work or a screen is not strength. Forget the verse, and you spend yourself on the woman God gave you. Remember it, and face the battle at her side.

Stay present with her. Tell her the pressure. Ask what the week has cost her before you explain a plan. Finish the hard conversation, or set the hour when you will. Lift her, and let her lift you.

Peter ties a husband’s life to the prayers he hopes God will hear.

“Likewise, ye husbands, dwell with them according to knowledge, giving honour unto the wife, as unto the weaker vessel, and as being heirs together of the grace of life; that your prayers be not hindered.”

1 Peter 3:7

Dwell with her according to knowledge. Learn the cost of this season, and give her honor. You are heirs together of the grace of life. A harsh tone, or a habit of winning against her, can hinder those prayers. Seek Christ together while the trouble is still in the room.

If you are not a husband yet, begin now. The habit of fighting alone, or of answering sharply, will come into a marriage with you. Tell the truth, stay when a talk is hard, and bring the fight to the Lord.

What the house receives

A wife whose husband stands with her can tell the truth. She should not have to guess, in a thin month or a sick week, whether he is for her. A man who stays and honors her tells her she is an heir with him, not the opponent.

A son watches whether his father treats his mother as an ally. A daughter watches whether strength stays or leaves her mother with the fear. They learn that trouble does not have to divide the people who love them.

Hard days still come home. What changes is the aim of the fight. Where a man stays, lifts the one who has fallen, and keeps Christ in the middle, the house takes the shape of a threefold cord.

Three things to do this week

  1. Name the battle and your side. If you have a wife, tell her whether it is stress, money, illness, or the conflict between you, and tell her you are with her. If you are not married, name the pressure to the Lord instead of making someone else the opponent.
  2. Ask what she is carrying before you add your need. Take one real weight from her this week, and do not keep score.
  3. Pray before you sleep. Thank God for one mercy, and ask help for the fight you named. If you have stood against her, say so first, and pray with her when she is willing.

A man who fights alone falls with no one to lift him. A man who fights his wife wounds the one he was called to cleave to. Stand with her, with Christ as the third strand, and you give your wife a partner, your children a steady house, and your home a cord that holds.

Who is my one? Begin with your wife, if the Lord has given you one, and stand with her in this battle. Then ask Him for one soul beyond your door. Read the mission, wear the Reach One bracelet as a reminder to reach one soul, or give to the work.

A prayer

Father, thank You that two are better than one, and that a threefold cord is not quickly broken. Forgive me for fighting alone and for turning against the one You gave me. Teach me to cleave to her and to honor her. Our wrestling is not against flesh and blood. If You have given children to this home, let them see a man who stays. Be the third strand in this home. In Jesus’ name, amen.
