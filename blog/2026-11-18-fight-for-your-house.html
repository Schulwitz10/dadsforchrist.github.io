Fight for Your House

Nehemiah 4:14 calls a man not to be afraid, but to remember the Lord and fight for his brethren, his sons, his daughters, his wife, and his house. He stands that post by being present and praying beside his wife, one hand on the work and the other on the watch, instead of withdrawing when pressure, discouragement, and spiritual attack come.

Men, pressure, discouragement, spiritual attack, and busyness can pull a man off the place where his wife needs him. This word is for husbands, for fathers, and for the man who will be one. Scripture in this post is quoted from the King James Version.

Nehemiah stood with a tired people on an unfinished wall. Enemies meant to stop the work, and he did not tell the men to hide.

“And I looked, and rose up, and said unto the nobles, and to the rulers, and to the rest of the people, Be not ye afraid of them: remember the Lord, which is great and terrible, and fight for your brethren, your sons, and your daughters, your wives, and your houses.”

Nehemiah 4:14

Be not afraid. Remember the Lord, who is great and terrible, and then fight. He named brethren, sons, daughters, wives, and houses in one charge. A man fights for them by staying at the post God gave him.

They worked with a weapon still in hand.

“They which builded on the wall, and they that bare burdens, with those that laded, every one with one of his hands wrought in the work, and with the other hand held a weapon. For the builders, every one had his sword girded by his side, and so builded. And he that sounded the trumpet was by me.”

Nehemiah 4:17–18

That picture belongs in a home. Show up, sit at the table, and keep watch while you build. Work without a watch leaves the gate open.

The battles you go through together

A husband and wife meet pressure, discouragement, spiritual attack, and busyness together. A man who is not married yet should learn that shape now. The attack comes as accusation, as cold distance, and as the urge to turn on each other. Busyness empties a chair and calls itself duty. Name the battle. Your wife is not the enemy.

“Finally, my brethren, be strong in the Lord, and in the power of his might. Put on the whole armour of God, that ye may be able to stand against the wiles of the devil. For we wrestle not against flesh and blood, but against principalities, against powers, against the rulers of the darkness of this world, against spiritual wickedness in high places. Wherefore take unto you the whole armour of God, that ye may be able to withstand in the evil day, and having done all, to stand.”

Ephesians 6:10–13

Your wife is not the adversary, and a child is not the adversary. The strength is given in the Lord. Put on the whole armour of God, withstand in the evil day, and having done all, stand.

To stand is to refuse withdrawal. Leaving the room, answering in one word, staying late, or giving the evening to a screen can look like peace. What the house receives is an empty post.

Present, praying, and at your post

Fight for your home by being present. Hear what her day cost, and stay in the hard talk instead of ending it by walking away.

Fight by praying. Set the Lord, who is great and terrible, in front of your weariness. Pray for her by name, and for the children by name if God has given them. When she is willing, pray a few plain sentences with her, so the fight is carried to God and not turned on her.

Fight by standing beside her. Do not leave her on the watch and call your absence rest. If you are not a husband yet, begin now. Stay when the talk is hard, and pray instead of disappearing. The man you practice being is the man a wife and a house will meet.

“And if one prevail against him, two shall withstand him; and a threefold cord is not quickly broken.”

Ecclesiastes 4:12

A husband and wife side by side are stronger than a man who fights alone, and stronger than a man who fights her. The third strand is the Lord. Keep Christ in the middle of the home. A threefold cord is not quickly broken, because the strength that holds is His.

What staying gives the house

A wife who is not left guessing can tell the truth about the week. A son sees a man who stays, and a daughter sees strength that is near without being harsh. If children are still ahead, let that habit be ready for them. Having done all, stand.

Three things to do this week

  1. Name the battle out loud. Tell your wife one pressure, or one place busyness pulled you away, and tell her you are with her. If you are not married, name that battle to the Lord, and do not make another person the enemy.
  2. Take one ordinary hour at your post. Set the screen down, come into the room, and stay. Ask what she is carrying, then do one piece of the house’s work with your own hands.
  3. Pray before the week ends. Pray once for the home by name. If she is willing, pray with her before you sleep. Ask for the whole armour of God, and ask Him to keep you from withdrawing.

Who is my one? Begin with your wife, if the Lord has given you one, and fight for that house by staying. Then ask Him for one soul past your door. Read the mission, wear the Reach One bracelet as a reminder to reach one soul, or give to the work.

A prayer

Father, You are great and terrible. Forgive the hours I left my post. Teach me to work with one hand and watch with the other. We wrestle not against flesh and blood. Clothe me with the whole armour of God. Make me present, praying, and standing beside the wife You have given or will give. Be the third strand, so this cord is not quickly broken. In Jesus’ name, amen.
