November 12, 2026

Stand Fast

First Corinthians 16:13–14 calls a man to be watchful, steady in the faith, courageous, and strong, and to do all of it in love. When his strength is gentle and his love is steady, his wife, his children, and his home are held.

Men, a house can feel a man’s strength and still miss his love, or feel his kindness and still miss his courage. Hardness is not strength. Softness that will not stand is not love. This word is for husbands, for fathers, and for the man who will be one. Scripture in this post is quoted from the King James Version.

“Watch ye, stand fast in the faith, quit you like men, be strong. Let all your things be done with charity.”

1 Corinthians 16:13–14

Paul sets four commands beside one rule. Watch. Stand fast in the faith. Be courageous, and be strong. Then let all your things be done with charity. In this letter, charity is love. Strength without that love is not the strength he names.

Watch

Watch ye. A man can be awake at work and asleep under his own roof. Peter says, “Be sober, be vigilant,” because the adversary walks about seeking whom he may devour (1 Peter 5:8). Watch your tone. Watch the weight your wife carries, if the Lord has given you one. Watch the child who has gone quiet, and the sin you have called strength. If that family is still ahead of you, watch now. The man you are becoming is the man they will meet.

Stand fast

Stand fast in the faith. The faith is the gospel you have received, not a mood that lasts through Sunday. A hard week will invite you to skip Scripture, drop prayer, and let the house run on hurry. Paul tells the church to be strong in the Lord, and in the power of his might (Ephesians 6:10). Open the Bible when you do not feel steady. Pray before you decide, and if you fail, come back the same day. A wife is steadied when her husband’s faith does not vanish with his mood. A child learns that the Lord is not a Sunday subject. If you are not yet a husband or a father, stand fast now.

Be strong

Quit you like men, be strong. Those old words call a man to courage. Courage at home tells the truth, says no to what would harm a wife or a child, confesses its own sin first, and stays in the room. Your strength is not your temper. God joins strength to love.

“For God hath not given us the spirit of fear; but of power, and of love, and of a sound mind.”

2 Timothy 1:7

Fear makes a man harsh, or it makes him hide. Power without love bruises a house. Love without power will not stand when wrong comes to the door. Ask for both, and use them in the next conversation.

With charity

Let all your things be done with charity. Watching, standing, courage, and strength are all to be done in love.

“Charity suffereth long, and is kind; charity envieth not; charity vaunteth not itself, is not puffed up,”

1 Corinthians 13:4

Longsuffering waits. Kindness does not need to prove itself. A strong man does not spend his strength on the last word. He uses it to serve. He can correct a child and still be kind, and he can disagree with his wife and still refuse bitterness.

What the home receives

When his strength is gentle and his love is steady, a wife can rest. She is not bracing for the next sharp word, and she is not the only courageous person in the house. A son learns that courage can tell the truth and still kneel. A daughter learns that a strong man can be safe. They are corrected, and they are not shamed. People in that home can tell the truth, because strength is present and it is not a threat.

When strength is hard and love is thin, a wife goes quiet and a child learns to read the room. When love never stands, nobody resists what is wrong. The people under your roof live with what you hold together.

The one

A strong man also goes after his one. The shepherd leaves the ninety and nine and goes after the lost sheep until he finds it, and there is joy in heaven over one sinner who repents (Luke 15:4–7). Who is my one? It may be your wife, a child who has gone quiet, or one man outside your door. Courage goes. Love stays until he is found, and it goes patiently and kindly. You cannot save a soul. Christ does. You can refuse to stay comfortable while that one is still lost.

Three things to do this week

  1. Tonight, name one place you have been asleep, and confess it to the Lord. If someone was hurt, say so before you sleep.
  2. This week, open the Scripture and pray at one ordinary hour the house can hear. If you live alone, keep the hour anyway.
  3. Before the week ends, do one hard thing in love. Have the talk you have delayed, or put the phone down and listen. Name your one, and take a step toward that person.

Ask the Lord for a man who is watchful, steady, courageous, and ruled by charity, and practice that before this day is over.

Who is my one? Begin with the person under your roof who needs both your strength and your love. Then ask the Lord for one soul beyond your door. Read the mission, wear the Reach One bracelet as a reminder to reach one soul, or give to the work.

A prayer

Father, You have not given me a spirit of fear, but of power, and of love, and of a sound mind. Forgive me where I have been harsh and called it strength, or soft and called it love. Make me watchful. Help me stand fast in the faith. Give me courage that serves, and let all I do be done with charity. Send me after the one. In Jesus’ name, amen.
