Pray With Her

Husbands and wives face real battles together. A husband who takes the lead in praying with his wife strengthens the marriage and the home.

Brother, the battles in a marriage are real, whether you are a husband today or you will be one. Money gets tight. A child is struggling. Both of you are tired, and a sharp word is still in the room. You face those fights together. A husband who takes the lead and prays with his wife strengthens the marriage and the home. Scripture in this post is quoted from the King James Version.

Paul tells a husband what his love is for.

“Husbands, love your wives, even as Christ also loved the church, and gave himself for it;”

Ephesians 5:25

Christ loved the church by giving Himself. He went first. A Christian husband loves his wife by going first where it costs him pride, and prayer is one of those places. It is easier to stay busy than to say, “Will you pray with me?” Leading in prayer is a man laying down his self-protection for his wife’s good.

Solomon saw what two can bear that one cannot.

“Two are better than one; because they have a good reward for their labour. For if they fall, the one will lift up his fellow: but woe to him that is alone when he falleth; for he hath not another to help him up. Again, if two lie together, then they have heat: but how can one be warm alone? And if one prevail against him, two shall withstand him; and a threefold cord is not quickly broken.”

Ecclesiastes 4:9–12

When one falls, the other can lift him. A shared life gives warmth a man cannot make alone, and a threefold cord is not quickly broken. Prayer is how you take the battle to God with your wife, instead of trying to hold the week by effort alone.

Jesus spoke of two who agree in asking.

“Again I say unto you, That if two of you shall agree on earth as touching any thing that they shall ask, it shall be done for them of my Father which is in heaven. For where two or three are gathered together in my name, there am I in the midst of them.”

Matthew 18:19–20

A husband and wife can be those two. After supper, or at the edge of the bed when the house is quiet, pray one shared request in His name, and leave room for her voice.

Peter joins a husband’s conduct to the prayers he hopes God will hear.

“Likewise, ye husbands, dwell with them according to knowledge, giving honour unto the wife, as unto the weaker vessel, and as being heirs together of the grace of life; that your prayers be not hindered.”

1 Peter 3:7

Learn what she is carrying, and give her honor. You are heirs together of the grace of life, and a husband’s prayers are hindered when he will not live that way. A harsh tone or a cold silence can clog the prayer you mean to pray at night.

When the battle is worry, Paul gives a clear road.

“Be careful for nothing; but in every thing by prayer and supplication with thanksgiving let your requests be made known unto God. And the peace of God, which passeth all understanding, shall keep your hearts and minds through Christ Jesus.”

Philippians 4:6–7

Bring the bill, the child, and the distance between you. Thank God in the same breath. His peace guards the hearts and minds of two people who have told Him the truth together.

Name the real fight before you take a side against her.

“For we wrestle not against flesh and blood, but against principalities, against powers, against the rulers of the darkness of this world, against spiritual wickedness in high places.”

Ephesians 6:12

Your wife is not the enemy. When a husband remembers that, he can turn from arguing at her to praying with her. Say it plainly: “We are on the same side. Let us ask God for help.”

If the two of you have rarely prayed together, start small. If you are not married yet, begin now by praying out loud for the home you may lead.

A way to begin this week

  1. Start with a short prayer at night. Thank God for one mercy and ask Him for one need. A minute, in a voice she can hear, is a real beginning.
  2. Ask her what to pray for. “What should we bring to the Lord tonight?” Pray her answer before you add your own.
  3. Pray when you are at odds. You do not have to feel close first. Before the light goes out, confess your part, and ask God to keep you from treating her as the opponent.
  4. Let the children see it. Pray where they can watch. A son learns what a husband is by watching, and a daughter learns what kind of strength she can trust.

You will miss nights. Pray the next one. A marriage grows stronger when a husband keeps returning to God with his wife, and a home grows stronger when everyone under the roof can tell that Jesus is welcome.

A prayer

Father, thank You for my wife, and that we are heirs together of the grace of life. Forgive me for leaving the praying to her, and for what has hindered my prayers. Teach me to love her as Christ loved the church, to honor her, and to agree with her in asking. Guard us with Your peace. Keep us from wrestling against each other, and hold this home. In Jesus’ name, amen.
