Teach Them Diligently

Faith is handed down at the dinner table, in the truck, and at bedtime, when a man talks of God’s words there and not only on Sunday.

Men, a family learns its faith in ordinary rooms. A child meets the words of God at the dinner table, in the truck, and at bedtime, not only in a pew. This word is for fathers, for husbands, and for the man who is neither yet. If God has given you a house, lead it in the small hours. If He has not, learn the habit now. Scripture in this post is quoted from the King James Version.

Moses set the pattern before Israel crossed into the land. Love for the Lord comes first. Then His words are taught through an ordinary day.

“And thou shalt love the LORD thy God with all thine heart, and with all thy soul, and with all thy might. And these words, which I command thee this day, shall be in thine heart: And thou shalt teach them diligently unto thy children, and shalt talk of them when thou sittest in thine house, and when thou walkest by the way, and when thou liest down, and when thou risest up.”

Deuteronomy 6:5–7

Teach them diligently. That is not one speech saved for Sunday. It is talk that keeps returning: when you sit in your house, when you walk by the way, when you lie down, and when you rise up. Supper is sitting in the house. The truck is walking by the way. Bedtime is lying down. Morning is rising up. Faith is handed down there, because that is where a family lives.

A man can share a roof and still miss these moments. Children learn what a father treats as normal. If God’s words show up only at church, a son may treat faith as a Sunday costume. If they show up over supper, on the drive, and before sleep, he learns that the Lord belongs to ordinary life. A daughter learns whether her father’s God is close enough to talk about when nobody is watching.

Paul gives fathers a charge for those same rooms.

“And, ye fathers, provoke not your children to wrath: but bring them up in the nurture and admonition of the Lord.”

Ephesians 6:4

Nurture is daily care: a meal, a calm correction, a man who stays. Admonition of the Lord points a child toward God. Both happen in the house. A harsh father can provoke wrath and make the name of Jesus feel like a threat. A present father corrects without crushing, and he lets a question at the table become the lesson.

Solomon calls that repeated work training.

“Train up a child in the way he should go: and when he is old, he will not depart from it.”

Proverbs 22:6

A path is worn by many steps, not by one visit a week. You do not hold the end of your child’s story. God does. Walk the way in front of him while he is in your house. If you are not a father yet, train your own heart on that path now.

A wife feels this presence too. When a husband talks of the Lord in the kitchen, she is not left to carry the faith of the home alone. Peter tells a husband how to live with her.

“Likewise, ye husbands, dwell with them according to knowledge, giving honour unto the wife, as unto the weaker vessel, and as being heirs together of the grace of life; that your prayers be not hindered.”

1 Peter 3:7

Heirs together. To dwell with her according to knowledge is to learn what she carries and share it. Thank God for the meal she cooked. Ask what is on her mind in the truck. End the day with a short word of Scripture. When a man leads there, his wife is honored, and the children see the Lord at the center of the marriage. When that honor is missing, Peter says, a husband’s prayers are hindered.

Jesus named this same love as the first commandment.

“And thou shalt love the Lord thy God with all thy heart, and with all thy soul, and with all thy mind, and with all thy strength: this is the first commandment.”

Mark 12:30

Love that wide will not fit in one Sunday hour. It follows a man into the house, down the road, into bed, and out again at dawn. Moses tied that love to talking of God’s words in those four places. The talking is how the love is handed on.

If you have been silent, start small. You need a true sentence, said where your people can hear it.

A way to begin this week

  1. At one meal, read Deuteronomy 6:5–7 aloud and ask where you saw the Lord’s care today.
  2. On one drive, turn the noise down and talk about one verse you are trying to obey.
  3. At bedtime, pray a short prayer with your child, or with your wife if the children are grown or still to come.
  4. In the morning, thank God in one sentence before the day gets loud.
  5. If you are not yet a husband or a father, keep the same four moments: sit with Scripture, speak it as you walk, pray when you lie down, and rise with thanks.

A prayer

Father, thank You for Your words and for the ordinary rooms where You told us to teach them. Forgive me for saving faith for Sunday. Make me a man who loves You with all my heart. Help me bring up my children in the nurture and admonition of the Lord, and honor my wife as an heir together of the grace of life. Put Your words in my mouth when I sit down, walk by the way, lie down, and rise up. In Jesus’ name, amen.
